Doña Fuego Braai Shoot
Sat 12 September 2026
Where

367 Ocean View Drive, Fresnaye, Cape Town.

Ocean View Drive is narrow and steep. Park tight against the kerb facing downhill, leave the driveway clear, and walk the last bit rather than blocking a neighbour. If it's full, drop your things at the top and go park below.

The light
≈ 17:30Golden hour opens — the whole afternoon builds to this
≈ 18:30Sunset over the Atlantic, straight off the deck
≈ 19:00Last usable light. Flash from here on

Times are for 12 September in Cape Town and shift a few minutes either way — I'll confirm on the day. Fresnaye faces due west, so nothing blocks the sunset. It also gets a wind after six: bring a jersey you don't mind being photographed in.

Bring
  • Yourself, on time, fed enough to last until the meat comes off.
  • Wardrobe per page 4. Two options if you're unsure — Clarissa will call it.
  • Sunglasses for the deck, a jersey for after sunset, swimming things.
  • Nothing to drink. Everything in frame has to be Doña Fuego, and there's plenty.
Before you shoot

There's a one-page release at the table. Everything shot on the day may end up on Doña Fuego's Instagram, in Checkers point-of-sale, or in the brand's press pack. Sign it before you pick up a camera, and if there's anything you'd rather not be in, say so early — it's an easy yes.

The one rule of the running order

Don't wait for a moment to be handed to you. If two people are laughing at the other end of the table, that's the shot — get up and take it. The best frame of the day will not be one anybody planned.

Frame budget

A disposable holds 27 frames across four hours. That's roughly one every nine minutes. Twelve are spoken for on page 6; the other fifteen are yours. Don't spend ten of them in the first half hour on the same cooler box.

Wear
  • Linen, cotton, crochet, denim. Texture photographs; polyester goes shiny under a flash.
  • Cream, bone, sand and butter as your base — they glow in the late sun.
  • Olive, forest and faded denim as your mid-tones.
  • Terracotta, coral, chilli and rust as your accent. Warm reds sit beautifully next to the label.
  • Straw hats, gold jewellery, leather sandals, bare feet, a good pair of sunglasses.
  • Old and worn beats new and stiff. A soft, faded shirt reads honest.
Leave at home
  • Head-to-toe black. A disposable flash turns it into a hole in the picture.
  • Bright white technical fabric and big white sneakers — they blow out and pull the eye off your face.
  • Neon and fluoro. They fight the label and win.
  • Tight stripes and small checks. They crawl and shimmer on the video camera.
  • Slogan tees, and any other brand's logo — especially another drink. That frame is unusable and we can't fix it afterwards.
  • Corporate golf shirts, hi-vis, anything you'd wear to a conference.
Pick one hero colour

Choose one strong piece — a chilli-red shirt, a coral dress, a terracotta jacket — and keep the rest of your outfit quiet. If ten people all arrive in cream we've shot a linen advert. Message Clarissa with your hero colour by Thursday and she'll spread us across the palette.

Small things that matter
  • Sunglasses off for portraits. A face without eyes is a wasted frame.
  • Big smartwatch off. Nothing dates a photo faster.
  • Bring a second top. Braai smoke and margarita both happen.

Your camera

Find your camera type
on the cover of this pack

Five cameras are running at this braai and none of them see the same afternoon. Read your one. Then read Liezl's and mine, so you know when to get out of the way.

Kodak disposable27 frames · film · flash
  • Wind on after every single shot. Thumb the wheel until it stops. No wind, no photo — this is the mistake everybody makes.
  • Stand at least a metre and a half back. Closer than that and it's a blur. Arm's-length selfies don't work: swap cameras and shoot each other instead.
  • Flash on in shade, indoors, and everywhere after 17:00. Hold the button until the little light glows, then shoot. It takes about eight seconds to charge — count them.
  • The flash reaches about three metres. Past that it does nothing, so don't waste one on the view.
  • Keep the sun behind you, not behind your subject, or you'll get a silhouette. The exception is shot 11, where a silhouette is the point.
  • Fingers off the top corners — that's the lens and the flash.
Kodak CharmeraTiny · digital · lo-fi
  • Charged, and a card in it if yours takes one, before you get a drink. Check it works on the first frame.
  • It loves light and hates shade. Shoot it hard between 15:00 and 18:30, then let the film cameras take over.
  • It costs nothing per frame — shoot ten for every one you'd take on film. Ten to one is a good ratio.
  • Lo-fi and slightly wrong is the whole point. Don't chase sharpness, chase the moment.
  • Get close where the disposables can't: hands, ice, labels, a face across the table.
  • Card or camera back to me at wrap so it gets dumped that night.
Underwater disposable27 frames · film · no flash
  • No flash. Bright daylight only. In shade it goes to mud, so keep it in the sun and finish it before the light drops.
  • You are the pool, the sea, the ice and the hose. Get it wet — that's what it's for.
  • Push it into the ice bin among the cans. Half-under, half-above the water is the frame everybody wants and almost nobody takes.
  • Still a metre and a half minimum, and still wind on every frame.
  • Water on the lens is a look, not a mistake. Wipe it only if you want the clean one.
Liezl · FujifilmHero stills
  • Liezl carries the shots that have to work: product in hand, faces, the table, the light.
  • If she's set something up, give her the room — then shoot the same moment from the side. Two angles on one moment beats two half-moments.
  • Don't walk through her frame to get to the fridge. Go around.
Johannes · SonyVintage video · sound
  • Long takes with sound rolling: pours, fire, laughing, the wind, the tongs.
  • If I'm rolling on you, carry on and don't look down the barrel unless I ask.
  • I'm the producer too. Anything you need — a can, a lift, a break — come to me.

The rest of the roll

Your call

Roughly fifteen frames
Nobody is checking

This is the half of the day we can't brief. The twelve get us the campaign; these get us the afternoon. If you're stuck, here are ten ways to spend them — but the best frame you take today won't be on this list.

  • The thing nobody else is looking at.
  • The person you came with, doing nothing in particular.
  • A frame with no people in it at all.
  • Your own feet, wherever they are.
  • Something ugly — a full ashtray, a chewed corn cob, a wet tea towel. Real beats pretty every time.
  • Whatever made you laugh in the last ten minutes.
  • The worst photo you can take on purpose.
  • Your camera pointed into a mirror or a window.
  • Someone else shooting someone else.
  • The last frame: whatever's in front of you when I call wrap. Don't think about it. Just press it.
Not negotiable

Eight rules of the day

  1. Everyone in a frame with a can is over 18 and reads 25 or older. No child, and nobody who could be mistaken for one, in a picture with alcohol or at the fire. That's the code South African drinks brands advertise under, and one frame breaking it costs us the whole set.
  2. Nobody appears drunk in a picture we use. Enjoying the afternoon, absolutely. Falling over, no. Pace yourself — it's four hours and there's water on the table all day.
  3. Nothing with car keys, driving, or a can anywhere near a car. Ever, in any frame, even as a joke.
  4. Only Doña Fuego in shot. No other drinks brands, no competitor cans, no wine labels, no branded glassware. Move it out of frame before you press the button — there's no fixing it afterwards.
  5. No phones in frame. Not on the table, not in a hand, not charging on the counter. Nothing dates a photograph faster.
  6. Shoot people the way you'd want to be shot. Every frame is fair game for Instagram and Checkers point-of-sale. If someone says no, that's a no, and it's the end of it.
  7. Sign the release at the table before you pick up a camera. One page, thirty seconds.
  8. Cameras back to me at 19:00, with your name on them. Films go to the lab Monday, digital cards get dumped the same night, and everybody gets the full set back.
And then

Every frame — the good, the blurred and the badly wound-on — comes back to you in a shared album. What Doña Fuego uses gets credited to whoever shot it. So put your name on your camera properly, and take the risky one.

Load-in & styling

Clarissa — styling & stock
Johannes — cameras & admin

Nothing on this list can be bought at 15:00. Everything lands at 367 Ocean View Drive by 12:00, and the fire is laid before the first guest walks in at 14:00.

Stock & cold chain
  • Margarita, Spicy Margarita, Paloma and Non-Alc Margarita — chilled cases plus a warm reserve to ice down through the day.
  • Hero cans: a dozen perfect cans set aside — no dents, no scuffs, no fingerprints. These are the only ones that go in front of Liezl.
  • Sealed 4-packs in the outer sleeve for the pack shot.
  • Branded cooler box plus two or three plain ones. Ice in bags — block for the bin, crushed for the glasses. More than you think, then one more bag.
  • A dedicated ice bin for shots 1 and the underwater camera. Nothing goes in it but ice, water and cans.
Bar
  • Short tumblers and mismatched glasses. Nothing with another brand on it.
  • Limes, lemons, grapefruit, fresh jalapeño, coriander, flaked salt for the rims.
  • Jugs of iced water on the table from the start, and visible all day.
Fire & food
  • Charcoal, firelighters, tongs, grid, brush, ash bucket. Fire laid by 13:30, lit at 15:45.
  • Boerewors, lamb chops, chicken sosaties, prawns. A vegetarian option that photographs as well as the meat does.
  • Salads built for colour: tomato, watermelon and feta, charred corn, green leaves.
  • Breads, butter, braai broodjies, condiments in bowls rather than bottles.
Table & props
  • Cream or natural linen cloth, wooden boards, enamel plates, cloth napkins.
  • Candles and hurricane lamps for after 18:45 — lit before the light goes, not after.
  • Straw hats, an old radio, a speaker and a playlist that runs four hours without a gap.
  • Two spare tops in the wardrobe palette, in case someone arrives in black or in another brand's logo.
Cameras (Johannes)
  • Ten cameras numbered 1–10 with tape and marker before anyone arrives, logged against names as they're handed out.
  • Two spare disposables. Somebody always finishes early or opens the back.
  • Charmeras charged and cards formatted the night before.
  • Sony: batteries charged, spare tape or card, and a working sound check before 14:00.
  • Liezl: spare batteries and cards on the table, not in a car.
  • A marked box by the door for cameras at wrap.
Admin
  • Ten printed copies of this pack, plus a spare.
  • Ten release forms and pens on the table by 13:45.
  • WhatsApp group live Thursday, wardrobe hero colours collected by Thursday night.
  • Sunscreen, bin bags, a first-aid kit, and an e-hailing budget for anyone who shouldn't drive.
After wrap

Films to the lab Monday for develop and scan. Digital cards dumped Saturday night. Full album back to everyone who shot, and a first selection to Clarissa by Friday 18 September.
