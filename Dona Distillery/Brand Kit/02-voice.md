# Voice & copy

## How we sound

Confident, warm, direct. Like a friend who knows their stuff and isn't
performing. Short sentences. South African English throughout —
*braai*, *stoep*, *cooler box*, *Checkers*, *lekker* if it's earned.

## Rules

**Specific over vague.** "Real tequila, lemon and lime juice" beats
"premium quality ingredients". Numbers beat adjectives. If you're
reaching for a superlative, you're missing a fact.

**Signature phrases once.** "No BS" is on the can. Use it where it lands
hardest and nowhere else in the same piece. Repeated, it stops meaning
anything.

**Never explain the joke.** If the picture says it, the caption doesn't
have to.

**No exclamation marks in brand voice.** One, occasionally, in a reply to
a customer. Never two.

**Write it as you'd say it.** Read the caption out loud. If you wouldn't
say it to someone holding a can, rewrite it.

## Words we use

tequila · real juice · cooler box · braai · ice · cold · Saturday ·
grab-and-go · no mixing · the good stuff

## Words we don't

premium · crafted · elevated · indulge · unleash · journey · curated ·
refreshingly · perfect for any occasion · sip · tantalising

Anything on that second list is a sentence that could belong to any
drink on the shelf.

## Caption shapes that work

**The flat statement.** One line, no setup.
> Two cans, one cooler box. That's the whole plan.

**The occasion.** Name the moment, let the picture do the rest.
> Fresnaye. Braai. Third round of palomas.

**The fact.** Specific, unshowy, quietly does the selling.
> Real tequila, real lemon and lime. Nothing else in it.

**The question that isn't a question.** For engagement without begging.
> Margarita or paloma. There's no wrong answer, but there is a right one.

## Length

Most captions: under 15 words. If it needs a paragraph, put the
paragraph in a carousel slide or a story, not under the picture.

## What we never do

- Never imply the drink makes you more attractive, successful or accepted
- Never mock people who aren't drinking
- Never use "responsibly" as a throwaway tag — if we say it, mean it
- Never make a health, medical or nutritional claim of any kind
- Never fake a customer quote or a review

## Afrikaans

Fine, and often better. Keep it conversational, not literary, and let
English business and product words stay English the way people actually
speak. Don't translate a caption twice — pick the language that fits the
picture.
