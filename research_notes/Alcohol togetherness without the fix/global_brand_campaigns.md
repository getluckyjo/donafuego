# Global alcohol brands: togetherness without the "fix" (case notes for Doña Fuego social)

Scope: global (non-SA) brands, 2013 onward, weighted to 2020–2026. Each case gives what is in the frame, what the drink does, copy quoted verbatim where verified, how responsible messaging is built in, and results/recognition. Anything not verified is marked in Gaps. Date of research: 16 October 2026.

Corrections to the brief, found while verifying:
- Heineken "Cheers to All" launched February 2020, not 2021. Its Kantar effectiveness award came in 2021.
- Aperol's togetherness campaign was "Together We Joy" (2019), not "Together We Can Cheers". I found no trace of a campaign by that name.
- Stella Artois's line is "Joie de Bière" (2018), a pun on "joie de vivre".
- Heineken's 2025 campaign is "Social Off Socials". "Social Off-Switch" isn't a verified name.
- The "Sapeurs" year is 2014. Guinness US's own site says 2004, which looks like a typo.

## 1. Heineken: how it puts connection, not the beer, at the centre

### Takeaway
Heineken's recurring move is to name the obstacle to good social time and make the brand the thing that removes it. The obstacles it picks are division, stereotypes, phones, work creep and drink-driving. The beer is present as the shared object in the scene (a bottle on the bar, a clink), never as the cause of the good time. Moderation is written into the brand itself rather than added as a legal line: 10% of Heineken media spend goes to responsible-drinking work, and the brand runs campaigns whose whole subject is drinking less.

### Cited Findings
**Responsible Marketing Code (the rulebook behind the work)**
- Heineken's code: "WE NEVER CLAIM THAT CONSUMING OUR BRANDS LEADS TO SOCIAL OR SEXUAL SUCCESS OR ENHANCED PERFORMANCE … WE NEVER PRESENT OUR BRANDS AS AN ENABLER FOR SEXUAL SUCCESS, A BETTER SOCIAL LIFE OR AS A PHYSICAL OR MENTAL PERFORMANCE ENHANCER." — [Heineken Responsible Marketing Code (2018 update, PDF)](https://heineken.hr/uploaded/OUR%20RESPONSIBLE%20MARKETING%20CODE_UPDATE%202018-1571403932626.pdf)
- The same code on moderation: "WE NEVER ENCOURAGE CONSUMERS TO DRINK LARGE AMOUNTS … INSTEAD WE ACTIVELY SEEK OPPORTUNITIES TO SHOW AND PROVE THAT DRINKING IN MODERATION IS COOL AND ASPIRATIONAL." It sets "10% of Heineken® media spend" for "the promotion of responsible drinking". It also notes that "alcoholic drinks are often used to set the tone for an occasion" but "we need to take care in how we represent those social environments and occasions". — [Heineken RMC PDF](https://heineken.hr/uploaded/OUR%20RESPONSIBLE%20MARKETING%20CODE_UPDATE%202018-1571403932626.pdf)

**"Worlds Apart" (#OpenYourWorld, UK, April 2017; Edelman / Publicis London)**
- In the frame: pairs of strangers with opposed views (feminism, climate change, and similar) build something together, usually a bar, without knowing each other's views. Their views are then revealed, and they choose to "walk away or stay and discuss their differences over beer". End line: "Open Your World". — [Contagious](https://www.contagious.com/en/article/news-and-views/insight-strategy-worlds-apart)
- Agency insight: "we are not as open as we think we are, but when we find common ground, over a beer, we are more likely to open up". It was validated with a Goldsmiths University study. — [Edelman case](https://www.edelman.com/work/heineken-worlds-apart)
- Results: "7.3% increase in beer sales in the UK during 12-week period following the campaign"; "80% of consumers said Heineken is a brand for them"; "78% felt closer affinity"; "More than 50 million views". — [Edelman](https://www.edelman.com/work/heineken-worlds-apart). Contagious reported 40m+ views at the time of writing, a Bronze Cyber Lion at Cannes and "91% of all sentiments are positive". — [Contagious](https://www.contagious.com/en/article/news-and-views/insight-strategy-worlds-apart)
- The Guardian framed it as a possible "antidote to the disastrous Pepsi/Kendall Jenner ad". — [The Guardian, 28 Apr 2017](https://www.theguardian.com/media/2017/apr/28/that-heineken-ad-does-it-land-with-the-audiences-other-beers-cannot-reach)

**"Cheers to All" (global, February 2020; Publicis Italy)**
- In the frame: a montage of bar staff serving "the wrong drink". The man who ordered a cocktail gets the Heineken and the woman who ordered the Heineken gets the cocktail. Everyone looks baffled, and the drinks are swapped. The joke is about the stereotype, not about drinking. — [Campaign Brief](https://campaignbrief.com/heineken-challenges-drinks-stereotypes-in-new-global-cheers-to-all-campaign-via-publicis-italy/)
- Heineken's Maud Meijboom: "We want to remind people around the world that everyone should feel free to enjoy the drink they prefer so we created 'Cheers to All'." — [Campaign Brief](https://campaignbrief.com/heineken-challenges-drinks-stereotypes-in-new-global-cheers-to-all-campaign-via-publicis-italy/)
- Recognition: top of Kantar's Creative Effectiveness Awards for 2020. Kantar's Daren Poole: "They address it in a way that's not preachy … with a humorous take on it. Rather than focusing on the fact women drink beer too, they highlight that men also drink cocktails." — [Adweek, Apr 2021](https://www.adweek.com/brand-marketing/these-were-the-most-effective-ads-of-2020-according-to-kantar/). Adweek says the campaign promoted "its alcohol-free lager", but Campaign Brief's launch report doesn't mention 0.0. The sources conflict on which product was featured.

**"The Closer" (US, June 2022; Publicis Worldwide)**
- Mechanic: a Bluetooth bottle opener that puts nearby laptops to sleep when it opens a bottle. It answered data showing "Employees have increased their average working day by 2.5 hours amid COVID-19" (NordVPN Teams). Line: "For a Fresher World". — [Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/heineken-bottle-opener-remote-work-apps/624680/)
- Heineken's help-page copy: "When you're thirsty for work-life balance, open a beer with The Closer. It will automatically close your work by putting your computer to sleep. Cheers." — [heineken.com (search excerpt)](https://www.heineken.com/global/en/campaigns/thecloser/thecloserhelp)

**"The Boring Phone" (with Bodega; launched Milan Design Week, 18 April 2024; LePub)**
- Insight: "9 out of 10 people doomscroll during nights out, and 40% admitted to missing meaningful moments because they were glued to their screens." Line: "there's more to social life when there's less on your phone." The beer: the phone is "best enjoyed with a cold Heineken in hand." — [The HEINEKEN Company newsroom](https://www.theheinekencompany.com/newsroom/heineken-boring-phone-turning-a-boring-phone-into-a-global-cultural-phenomenon/)
- Bruno Bertelli (LePub): "'The Boring Phone' is ultimately a metaphor for a better social life." Also: "The hands with beers clinking are also very strategic: it is Heineken that is connecting people in this case." And: "At the heart of Heineken is to make social life more refreshing and rewarding." — [LBB, 24 Apr 2024](https://lbbonline.com/news/dialling-down-distractions-inside-heinekens-boring-phone-with-bruno-bertelli)
- Results: "In 2024, Heineken® shattered records with 9.5 billion impressions – the highest in its history". The Boring Phone "generated 2,210 press mentions, with 51 appearing in gold-tier outlets", and creators "produced 53 organic pieces of content, driving over 43,000 engagements". — [HEINEKEN newsroom](https://www.theheinekencompany.com/newsroom/heineken-boring-phone-turning-a-boring-phone-into-a-global-cultural-phenomenon/). Silver at Eurobest 2024 — [Love The Work (Cannes Lions)](https://www.lovethework.com/en/awards/results/eurobest?year=2024&entry_type_id=11)

**"Social Off Socials" (2025, with Joe Jonas; Heineken's largest campaign of 2025)**
- Creators are taken offline. The hook: "What if influencers had to hack it in the real world?" The campaign cites a OnePoll study of 17,000 adults: the average person spends "approximately 5 hours and 48 minutes per day looking at their device". — [Adweek, 29 Apr 2025](https://www.adweek.com/creativity/heineken-and-joe-jonas-take-socializing-offline-to-tackle-digital-burnout/)
- Heineken's page claims "people look 79% less at phones during the night out" and invites people "to be #SocialOffSocials". — [heineken.com (search excerpt; page age-gated)](https://www.heineken.com/global/en/campaigns/social-off-socials/). The US page frames it as "a celebration of being present - for the cheers, the laughs, the awkward dance moves, and the spontaneous stories". — [heineken.com/us (search excerpt)](https://www.heineken.com/us/en/campaigns/socialoffsocials)

**Moderation as the creative subject**
- "Moderate Drinkers Wanted" (January 2016; Publicis Italy; 30 markets, per [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/266315/heineken-launches-global-campaign-telling-men-to-d.html?edition=89302)). Women walk through bars singing Bonnie Tyler's "I Need A Hero" as men "crumple into drunken stupors". It "closes with a suave man impressing the woman behind the bar by turning down another bottle of Heineken." Research cited: "75%" of millennials "limit how much alcohol they drink on the majority of nights out"; "36%" had suffered "social shaming" over drunk photos; "69%" limit drinking to avoid "loss of control". — [Business Insider, 12 Jan 2016](https://www.businessinsider.com/heineken-launches-moderate-drinkers-wanted-ad-2016-1)
- "When You Drive, Never Drink" started in 2016 as the responsible-consumption platform for Heineken's F1 sponsorship, with Sir Jackie Stewart. Alongside it ran signage in nightlife areas and car parks, non-alcoholic drinks at nightspots, and "driver's menus". — [Edelman](https://www.edelman.com/work/heineken-wydnd)
- The 2020 version stars Keke and Nico Rosberg in a father-son rivalry (tennis, fishing). It drew on research among 10,000 drivers in 10 markets, including South Africa. Heineken's Gianluca Di Tondo: "while people don't set out to drink and drive, often when we're at the point of decision making, our good intentions falter in the face of temptation." The company "directs 10% of media spend to promoting responsible drinking … with a special focus on When You Drive, Never Drink." — [HEINEKEN newsroom, 26 Feb 2020](https://www.theheinekencompany.com/newsroom/heineken-unveils-new-when-you-drive-never-drink-campaign-featuring-two-formula-1-world-champions/)

**Heineken 0.0: the zero as an equal, not a penalty**
- "Now You Can" (US, January 2019; Publicis). Four spots show people drinking what look like regular beers in "inappropriate situations like work settings", such as a business presentation, a film set, a petrol station and a car park, then revealing it's 0.0. CMO Jonnie Cahill: "It's not about a replacement strategy; it's complementary … We see this as an 'and,' not an 'instead of,' purchase." — [MediaPost, 11 Jan 2019](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/330394/now-you-can-ads-intro-no-alcohol-heineken-00-in.html?edition=112434). A local site's line: "Ice-cold beer post-hot-yoga class? Now you can." — [heineken.com/jm (search excerpt)](https://www.heineken.com/jm/en/campaigns/heineken-0-0)
- "0.0 Reasons Needed" (January 2025; LePub): "Let's face it—people think there's always a reason behind a non-alcoholic beer. Dieting? Driving? Working late? Wrong." — [heineken.com (search excerpt)](https://www.heineken.com/global/en/campaigns/zero-reasons); [Adweek, 3 Jan 2025](https://www.adweek.com/creativity/in-heineken-00s-new-ads-it-doesnt-matter-why-youre-going-dry/). Sister execution "Going Dry? Doesn't Matter Why". — [Ads of the World](https://www.adsoftheworld.com/campaigns/going-dry-doesn-t-matter-why)

### Inferences
- The pattern is enemy, then remedy. The enemy is never boredom, stress or a bad day (all prohibited). It's something that blocks connection: division, stereotypes, screens, work spilling over. The remedy is people being present, and the beer is just in the room. Doña Fuego can do the same with braai and beach enemies such as phones at the fire, the group chat that never turns into a plan, and the one oke who never brings anything.
- Even Heineken's best work sits close to the line. Bertelli's "it is Heineken that is connecting people" and Edelman's "over a beer, we are more likely to open up" conflict with Heineken's own code ("never … an enabler for … a better social life") and with Diageo's ban on implying drink helps "overcome inhibitions" (section 3). The film gets away with it because the beer is offered after the connection is made, as a choice to stay. For Doña Fuego: the can arrives once the people are already there and the plan has happened. It should never be what made them talk.
- "The Closer" ties opening a beer to the end of work. It's framed as work-life balance, but the UK ASA treated a similar "rest of the day is to be endured until drinking time" message from Jack Daniel's as a breach (section 7). It's an edge case to avoid copying.

### Gaps
- Heineken Silver: I found no reliable campaign source on how it frames social occasions; the search returned nothing usable.
- Full verbatim script for "Worlds Apart": the exact on-screen question wasn't verified. Contagious only paraphrases it as stay "and discuss their differences over beer".
- Results for "Cheers to All" beyond the Kantar ranking (sales, reach) weren't found. Cannes awards for The Boring Phone could only be checked through an individual copywriter's LinkedIn ("Bronze (x2)"), which is too weak a source to cite.

## 2. Guinness: "Made of More", "Empathy", "Sapeurs", Guinness Clear, 0.0, "Lovely Day", and "split the G"

### Takeaway
In its character era (2012–2019, "Made of More") Guinness made the people's choices the hero and kept the pint as the closing reward of a story about loyalty or self-expression. The 2023–2026 "Lovely Day" revival moved to plain sunshine and friends, puts Guinness 0.0 next to the stout as an equal, and is backed by IPA effectiveness evidence. Its responsible-drinking work (Guinness Clear, 0.0 on St Patrick's Day) is branded and funny, not preachy. On the "split the G" UGC game, the evidence shows Diageo stayed hands-off and only gave a "rule" when journalists asked, which fits its code ban on speed-drinking games.

### Cited Findings
**"Empathy" / wheelchair basketball (US, 2013; BBDO)**
- In the frame: friends playing wheelchair basketball. At the end all but one stand up, revealing they used chairs so their friend could play. Then they go to the bar together. Voiceover: "The choices we make reveal the true nature of our character." — [Forbes, 17 Dec 2013](https://www.forbes.com/sites/jacquelynsmith/2013/12/17/the-most-unforgettable-ad-campaigns-of-2013/) (Forbes: "While the six friends enjoy a few post-game beers, the voiceover says…")
- A disability writer criticised it as "Just One of the Guys". — [Emily Ladau, Sept 2013](https://emilyladau.com/2013/09/just-one-of-the-guys/)

**"Sapeurs" (2014; AMV BBDO; director Nicolai Fuglsig)**
- The opening line: "In life, you cannot always choose what you do, but you can always choose who you are." — [BBC News](https://www.bbc.com/news/blogs-magazine-monitor-25783245); [Ethan Zuckerman](https://ethanzuckerman.com/2014/01/31/sharp-suits-warm-beer-guinness-sapeurs-and-the-seduction-of-congolese-fashion/)
- Guinness describes it as culminating in "a brightly coloured and social affair, which sees the community unite and celebrate". The same page dates the ad to 2004, which looks like a typo for 2014. — [Guinness US](https://www.guinness.com/en-us/our-craft/guinness-advertising)

**Guinness Clear (GB and Ireland, from 1 February 2019, Six Nations; AMV BBDO; director Tom Kuntz)**
- Mechanic: tap water rebranded and launched like a product, with a TV ad, #GuinnessClear on social, branded water fonts and sampling teams at Home Nations stadiums. Copy: "It's just a Guinness that happens to be made with 100% H20, that's all." — [LBB](https://lbbonline.com/news/amv-bbdo-and-guinness-rebrand-tap-water-to-guinness-clear)
- Mark Sandys (Guinness): "We think the light-hearted approach … will drive conversation and put responsible drinking to front of mind … We want to make sure that ordering water, no matter the context, is an active and positive choice." — [LBB](https://lbbonline.com/news/amv-bbdo-and-guinness-rebrand-tap-water-to-guinness-clear)
- Guinness's own page: "Make It a Night You'll Remember … We created Guinness Clear to help you feel the benefits of spacing your drinks with water." — [guinness.com](https://www.guinness.com/en/beers/guinness-clear-responsible-drinking). Cannes Lions described it as "triggering genuine behaviour change among rugby fans". — [Cannes Lions (Facebook excerpt)](https://www.facebook.com/Cannes.Lions.Festival.of.Creativity/videos/stout-brand-guinness-removed-the-stigma-of-drinking-water-it-rebranded-it-as-gui/447330367008512/)

**Guinness 0.0 on St Patrick's Day (Ireland; Marketing Week Award 2024)**
- Copy: "Make it a St. Patrick's Day to Remember"; pop-up pub "0.0'Donoghue's"; "Buy 1, get 0.0 free". Guinness 0.0 was on tap at partner venues, with sampling, a Tesco promotion and a Snapchat AR lens. Insight: for some, St Patrick's Day "had become a day of shame". — [Marketing Week, Jan 2025](https://www.marketingweek.com/guinness-alcohol-free-st-patricks-day/)
- Results: social mentions of Guinness 0.0 rose 74%, Irish supermarket sales of 0.0 rose 50%, responsibility scores improved, and it won the 2024 Marketing Week Award for Consumer Goods. — [Marketing Week](https://www.marketingweek.com/guinness-alcohol-free-st-patricks-day/)

**"Lovely Day for a Guinness" revival (2023–2026)**
- The revived summer work centres on "a group of friends trying to enjoy the sunshine and a pint together". Guinness and AMV BBDO won the IPA Effectiveness Awards' President's Prize for Next Generation Brand Building (October 2024). — [Creative Salon](https://creative.salon/articles/features/guinness-brand-effectiveness-amv-bbdo)
- Ireland "Lovely Days" case (2024): the problem was "over ten years of continual share decline" and dependence on "45+ males during the winter months". Results: penetration among 18–34s rose "from 17.7% in 2019 to 23.5% in 2024"; "+6% more female drinkers"; "No.1 brand share in summer, reversing a 10-year share decline"; 'Great for summer occasions' up 5%. — [The Drum awards case](https://www.thedrum.com/awards-case-study/how-guinness-redefined-summer-with-a-vibrant-transformation-campaign)
- US "A Lovely Day" (April 2025) shares "50 real stories illustrating the role of Guinness and Guinness 0.0 in celebrations", with "474 million impressions and counting". — [Diageo](https://www.diageo.com/en/news-and-media/stories/2025/guinness-lovely-day-in-the-united-states). Site copy: "50 states, one incredible truth: when you're with your people, it's always a lovely day." — [guinness.com/en-us (search excerpt)](https://www.guinness.com/en-us/our-craft/lovely-day). PR copy: a "5,057-mile journey to every single state … capturing what unites people over a pint." — [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/guinness-spotlights-lovely-days-across-the-country-in-new-campaign-302425618.html)
- 2024 out-of-home "LoVely" puts the harp in place of the "v". — [Mediastreet.ie](https://mediastreet.ie/this-clever-design-trick-by-guinness-means-it-can-modernise-without-needing-a-new-logo/)

**"Split the G" (UGC game, about 2023–2025)**
- The game: drink so that your "first sip" leaves the foam line across the middle of the "G" on the glass. Diageo's only verified public comment is the "official" rule, given to a PR spokesperson rather than run as a campaign: "The only correct way to Split the G would be when your first uninterrupted sip lines up with the line on the G and E." The responsible-drinking caveat in that article came from the third party, not from Guinness. — [Express, 14 Mar 2025](https://www.express.co.uk/news/uk/2027130/guinness-official-rule-pub-game-splitting-the-g); [The Sun](https://www.thesun.co.uk/fabulous/34062976/split-the-g-guinness-pint-pub-game-rules-explained/)
- Commentators say the trend "didn't cost Guinness a single marketing penny" and credit the existing ritual of the "two-part pour (a precisely timed 119.5-second ritual)". — [Mediashower](https://www.mediashower.com/blog/guinness-splittheg/) (marketing blog, low authority)
- Diageo blamed shortages on "exceptional consumer demand", reported as partly due to "Split the G". — [LinkedIn News](https://www.linkedin.com/news/story/guinness-tapped-amid-split-the-g-7098018/)
- Diageo's code (section 3) bars sponsoring "drinking games that have speed incentives, or that require undue quantities of alcohol to be consumed". — [Diageo Marketing Code 2023](https://www.diageo.com/~/media/Files/D/Diageo-V2/Diageo-Corp/our-business/corporate-governance/code-of-business-conduct/policies-and-standards/diageo-marketing-code-2023.pdf)

### Inferences
- Guinness has two scripts. The character script (Empathy, Sapeurs) says the people's choices are the point and the pint is how they mark them. The Lovely Day script says the day and the company are the point, with Guinness or 0.0 alongside. Both keep the drink as the mark of the moment, never what caused it. "When you're with your people, it's always a lovely day" is close to a model line for Doña Fuego: the people make the day.
- On split the G, the evidence points to a hands-off approach: no paid push, a neutral rule when asked, and the pour ritual as the safe shareable asset rather than the gulp. A UGC drinking game shouldn't be amplified, but the ritual around it can be celebrated. For Doña Fuego: celebrate the crack of the can, the clink and the cooler-box reveal, and never repost "who can finish it fastest".

### Gaps
- I couldn't verify whether official Guinness Instagram or TikTok accounts posted or reposted split-the-G content, or whether Guinness issued a formal statement on it.
- Guinness Christmas work (2023–2025) wasn't covered: searches didn't surface a reliable source for copy or results.
- I didn't find a verified results figure for Guinness Clear. A Marketing Society case PDF exists ([PDF](https://www.marketingsociety.com/sites/default/files/2020-07/REDACTED%20AMVBBDO%20Guiness%20Clear_Redacted.pdf)) but wasn't read.
- The full "Empathy" voiceover (the lines before the closing sentence) wasn't verified, and nor was "Made of More" copy beyond the platform name.

## 3. Diageo: Johnnie Walker, Smirnoff, Don Julio, DRINKiQ and the Diageo Marketing Code

### Takeaway
Diageo's marketing code has the most useful single sentence for Doña Fuego. Brands "may be portrayed as the perfect choice for already energetic or relaxing occasions, but we will not imply that they are the catalyst for any change". The code also bans implying drink helps people "overcome inhibitions, shyness or social barriers", bans mocking people who refuse, and treats rapid shots and speed games as off-limits. Diageo's brand work follows suit. Smirnoff sells inclusion and "the collective", Don Julio sells love of craft and Mexico, Johnnie Walker sells personal progress, and DRINKiQ carries the harm messages.

### Cited Findings
**Diageo Marketing Code (updated January 2023), verbatim**
- §06: "Not imply that alcohol is the catalyst for any change in mood or state" … "h. Our alcohol brands may be portrayed as the perfect choice for already energetic or relaxing occasions, but we will not imply that they are the catalyst for any change in energy, state of alertness or states of mental or physical relaxation." — [Diageo Marketing Code 2023 (PDF)](https://www.diageo.com/~/media/Files/D/Diageo-V2/Diageo-Corp/our-business/corporate-governance/code-of-business-conduct/policies-and-standards/diageo-marketing-code-2023.pdf)
- §07: marketing "will not imply that drinking is necessary to obtain social, professional, educational, athletic, financial or other success, nor to solve social, personal, or physical problems, nor to overcome inhibitions, shyness or social barriers, or to be socially accepted." Brands may be shown "as part of a social or romantic setting". — [DMC 2023](https://www.diageo.com/~/media/Files/D/Diageo-V2/Diageo-Corp/our-business/corporate-governance/code-of-business-conduct/policies-and-standards/diageo-marketing-code-2023.pdf)
- §04: "Marketing that depicts responsible drinking as a relaxed, sociable and enjoyable part of life has a role to play in promoting a responsible approach to drinking." Also: "We will not depict people drinking heavily or rapidly" and "We will not sponsor promotions involving drinking games that have speed incentives". On shots: "A single shot may be enjoyed responsibly as part of an occasion, but we will not depict or encourage rapid or multiple shot consumption by individuals." Plus "We will not promote our brands with irresponsible cocktail names." — [DMC 2023](https://www.diageo.com/~/media/Files/D/Diageo-V2/Diageo-Corp/our-business/corporate-governance/code-of-business-conduct/policies-and-standards/diageo-marketing-code-2023.pdf)
- §02: "We will not present abstinence from or moderation with alcohol in a negative manner, nor imply that it is wrong or foolish to refuse a drink, even in a humorous manner." — [DMC 2023](https://www.diageo.com/~/media/Files/D/Diageo-V2/Diageo-Corp/our-business/corporate-governance/code-of-business-conduct/policies-and-standards/diageo-marketing-code-2023.pdf)
- Non-alcoholic variants of alcohol brands "Must avoid any appeal on the grounds of stated health, dietary or functional benefits" but "may be marketed as thirst quenching, hydrating or refreshing". Shareable content "should include a Forward Advice Notice (FAN)" telling people not to forward it to anyone under the legal purchase age. — [DMC 2023](https://www.diageo.com/~/media/Files/D/Diageo-V2/Diageo-Corp/our-business/corporate-governance/code-of-business-conduct/policies-and-standards/diageo-marketing-code-2023.pdf)

**Smirnoff: "We're Open" (about 2014–2018) to "WE DO WE" (2023 onward)**
- "We're Open" was a £4.5m UK push — [The Grocer](https://www.thegrocer.co.uk/news/smirnoffs-were-open-campaign-aims-for-inclusiveness/518036.article). It later ran on TV to "shed light on the deaf community" — [Marketing Week](https://www.marketingweek.com/smirnoff-takes-a-personal-approach-as-it-sheds-light-on-the-deaf-community-with-were-open-tv-push/)
- The November 2017 LGBTQ+ film with the LGBT Foundation starred Honey Dijon, Lucy Fizz and Xnthony. Diageo's Anita Robinson: "Nightlife is a place where everyone should feel welcomed for who they are … Smirnoff's brand's purpose is to move the world to be more inclusive." — [The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/news/smirnoff-continues-focus-inclusivity-latest-star-studded-lgbtq-were-open-campaign)
- "WE DO WE" (global, 15 June 2023; McCann New York) celebrates "the magic of 'we' that's created when different people, ingredients, and flavors come together". ### Inferences
- The "already" test from Diageo §06h is the cleanest rule for Doña Fuego: show a braai or beach that's already good and put Fuego in it. Never show the before-and-after, where a flat afternoon becomes a party once the cans come out.
- "WE DO WE" shows a brand can talk about loneliness and togetherness without breaking the rules. It does so by celebrating the collective and mixing ("different people, ingredients, and flavors"), not by promising the drink cures isolation. The headline framing ("addressing the loneliness epidemic") is still risky ground under UK CAP rule 18.6 (section 7), so Doña Fuego shouldn't borrow the loneliness angle.
- Don Julio grounds celebration in place, people and craft. The local equivalent is grounding Fuego in SA occasions (braai, beach, Saturday afternoon) and in the product truth ("real tequila, lemon & lime juice"). Neither is a mood claim.

## 5. Tequila brands and tequila RTDs: margarita ritual and friendship rather than shots

### Takeaway
The tequila brands that do this well steer away from shots in three ways. They build the brand story on friendship (Casamigos is literally "house of friends"). They champion the margarita as a crafted, shared cocktail (Patrón's "Margarita of the Year", Cuervo's National Margarita Day). Increasingly, they talk ingredient truth (Patrón's 2026 "three ingredients" campaign), which is almost exactly Doña Fuego's "real tequila, lemon & lime juice" proposition. RTD tequila brands (Cutwater, Onda) sell bringing the bar to the occasion and fruit-juice credentials. The 2026 Cuervo "Summer Fridays" push shows how easily margarita promotions slide into "escape work" framing.

- The tequila brands that avoid the shots image do it by making the margarita a crafted, shared cocktail, which matches Doña Fuego's three flavours exactly. "Real tequila, lemon & lime juice" is a product truth in Patrón's style, not a mood promise, so it's the safest hero message available. Diageo's code shows how carefully the industry treats shots: one shot "may be enjoyed responsibly as part of an occasion", but never "rapid or multiple" ones.
- Cutwater's "bring the bar to where you are" matches the cooler-box idea: the can is the thing you bring. Copy about Fuego should be about bringing, packing, sharing and sorting the cooler box.
- Cuervo's "shut their laptops and start their weekends early" is a reward-for-work frame. In the UK it would invite the Jack Daniel's "skip to the good part" reasoning (section 7). For Doña Fuego, celebrate the Saturday itself, not the escape from the week.

It's the language of craving and dependency, aimed at the individual, and the drink becomes the need. ## 7. Getting it wrong: posts ruled against or criticised for framing alcohol as coping, reward, pick-me-up or cure

### Takeaway
Regulators consistently reject the same moves, often in posts the brand thought were jokes or "about friends": alcohol as a cure for boredom, mid-week, short days, Dry January or lockdown; alcohol as emotional support or self-care; alcohol as "got your back"; and visible energy or confidence lifts after the alcoholic drink appears. Humour doesn't protect a post, and nor does a "we meant friendship" defence. The UK ASA has ruled against Diageo (Captain Morgan), Brown-Forman (Jack Daniel's) and BrewDog, so big brands aren't exempt.

### Cited Findings
**The UK rules applied (CAP Code, non-broadcast)**
- Rule 18.6: "Marketing communications must not imply that alcohol might be indispensable or take priority in life or that drinking alcohol can overcome boredom, loneliness or other problems." — [ASA: Boredom and loneliness](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/alcohol-boredom-and-loneliness.html)
- Rule 18.7: "Marketing communications must not imply that alcohol has therapeutic qualities. Alcohol should not be portrayed as capable of changing mood, physical condition or behaviour". ASA guidance: "Claims such as 'chill with your mates' may be acceptable depending on context" but not if "the ad implies alcohol can make drinkers more 'chilled'". Marketers must not "imply that anyone might need (as opposed to merely enjoy) an alcoholic drink." — [ASA: Therapeutic claims](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/alcohol-therapeutic-claims.html)

**"Hump day" and boredom**
- Diageo (Captain Morgan), 2014: a pirate raising a glass with the text "Wednesday. I'm declaring war on mid-week boredom". The ASA found it suggested "drinking could alleviate boredom". — [ASA](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/alcohol-boredom-and-loneliness.html) (ruling: [Diageo Great Britain Ltd, 23 Jul 2014](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/Diageo-Great-Britain-Ltd-A14-270730.html))
- Jack Daniel's (Brown-Forman), February 2024: "Shorter days mean we can skip to the good part." The advertiser said the message was about "spending more time with friends". The ASA "felt that the ad implied that the rest of the day was tedious and to be endured until drinking time began." — [ASA](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/alcohol-boredom-and-loneliness.html) (ruling: [Brown Forman, 28 Feb 2024](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/brown-forman-beverages-europe-ltd-a23-1223370-brown-forman-beverages-europe-ltd.html))

**"Got your back" / the drink as the solution**
- BrewDog Wingman, August 2025: "BREWDOG. ALWAYS GOT YOUR BACK… Some things in life go AWOL – WiFi fails, the weather turns hostile, and your buddy's 'five minutes' turns into a full-scale delay. But Wingman? Wingman stands firm." The ASA accepted the ad evoked "camaraderie" but found "the scenarios presented were real-life problems … and the beer was a solution to overcoming those problems". — [ASA](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/alcohol-therapeutic-claims.html) (ruling: [Brewdog Plc, 6 Aug 2025](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/brewdog-plc-a25-1291535-brewdog-plc.html))

**Self-care and emotional support**
- La Vieille Ferme (TikTok), May 2026: three wine boxes labelled "One to resurrect my soul", "One for emotional support" and "One to erase today", with mental-health copy. The ASA held that it "implied that drinking alcohol could enhance one's mood and emotional well-being". — [ASA](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/alcohol-therapeutic-claims.html) (ruling: [Perrin et Fils, 20 May 2026](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/perrin-et-fils-sas-g26-1334485-perrin-et-fils-sas.html)). The headline was "Finding Balance: Emotional Support and Self Care". — [The Drinks Business](https://www.thedrinksbusiness.com/2026/05/asa-bans-la-vieille-ferme-tiktok-wine-advert/)
- Drake & Morgan, 2019: a quiz offering a "potion to match their emotion" for people feeling "overstretched" or "low-spirited", with "give yourself a boost". Upheld. — [ASA](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/alcohol-therapeutic-claims.html)
- Blackrose (Facebook), 2019: "If at first you don't succeed, try drinking a bottle of prosecco, you'll be surprised at how much less you'll care". Upheld; the parody didn't save it. — [ASA](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/alcohol-therapeutic-claims.html)

**Before-and-after energy on Instagram and TikTok**
- Muff Liquor Company with Laura Whitmore (Instagram/TikTok, posted July 2022, ruled 2023). She dances "more confidently and enthusiastically" as her drink changes from non-alcoholic drinks to "Muff and Tonic", with the caption "If drinks were dance moves". The ASA found this implied the drink "would enhance their confidence and improve their mood" and that "sobriety was boring". — [RPC](https://www.rpclegal.com/snapshots/advertising-and-marketing/spring-2023/asa-slams-social-media-post-for-breaching-rules-on-alcohol-advertising/)

**Lockdown and Dry January as problems to drink through**
- Reserve Ltd email, 2021: "Friend, forget Dry January … we thought you might need all the help you could get … Lockdown 3.0 - a 6-bottle mixed case … A survival kit, if you will." Upheld, despite mentioning non-alcoholic options. — [ASA](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/alcohol-boredom-and-loneliness.html)
- Instagram ad, 2025 (advertiser not named in the source): "Doing Dry January? One won't hurt, right?" The ASA said it tempted Dry January participants to drink "to avoid the boredom of abstinence", and that "one won't hurt, right?" was "associated with peer pressure". — [DLA Piper, Aug 2025](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2025/08/asa-rulings-summary-mind-your-ads)
- Australia, April 2020 pandemic posts: "A bottle a day keeps the iso boredom away", "iso six pack", "survival kits", "Feeling depresso about being Iso this Easter". ABAC found Thirsty Camel in breach over two posts joking about heavy drinking in isolation. FARE's Caterina Giorgi: "I was absolutely bombarded with alcohol social media marketing that mentioned isolation or coping with stress and anxiety by using alcohol." ABAC chief adjudicator Michael Lavarch: "You can't suggest that the consumption of alcohol is an aid to relaxation or is necessarily beneficial." — [ABC News, 16 Apr 2020](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2020-04-16/coronavirus-themed-alcohol-marketing-sparks-call-for-ban/12151912). FARE also reported "71%" of alcohol ads in its sample "explicitly or implicitly referenced the COVID-19 pandemic". — [FARE](https://fare.org.au/alcohol-ad-every-35-seconds-during-covid-19/)

**Other relevant ABAC (Australia) findings**
- "A Heineken 3 Instagram post demonstrated how alcohol marketing cannot show alcohol as causing or contributing to the achievement of personal, social or sexual success." A Liquorland Spotify ad breached the standard against suggesting alcohol "may create or contribute to a significant change in mood". — [The Shout, 2018](https://theshout.com.au/alcohol-advertisers-meeting-new-abac-rules/)
- ABAC panel, 2025: "The standard does not allow alcohol to be shown or implied as the cause of a change in mood from one state to a better, improved state". — [ABAC Determination 130/25 (excerpt)](http://www.abac.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/130-25-FINAL-Determination-24-September-2025.pdf)

**"Mummy juice" and #winemom**
- An Instagram study found alcohol "presented as the saviour that allowed them to cope with their domestic duties". The authors warn it "problematically frame[s] alcohol as the solution" for women "barely getting by". — [Substance Abuse: Research and Treatment (PMC), 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8114293/). See also the "mommy juice" critique — [The Kitchn](https://www.thekitchn.com/mom-wine-culture-268335). During COVID, research reported advertisers "using social media to prey on parents and encourage drinking as a…" (excerpt cut off) — [MJA / Medical Xpress, 2020](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2020-08-covid19-cocktails-tighter-alcohol-advertising.html)

**Humour and memes**
- Fireball, 2013: "I'd dump my boyfriend for more Fireball cinnamon whisky". The ASA rejected the "written in jest" defence. — [ASA](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/alcohol-boredom-and-loneliness.html)
- Drink Doctor memes, 2017: "People are not addicted to alcohol or drugs, they are addicted to escaping reality". Upheld even though they were "popular internet memes". — [ASA](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/alcohol-boredom-and-loneliness.html)
- Novielo, 2023: a "PRESCRIPTION ALCOHOL" sticker ("DOSE: Drink one 175ml dose twice daily"). Upheld as linking alcohol to medicine. — [ASA](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/alcohol-therapeutic-claims.html)

### Inferences
- "Get your Fuego fix" hits several of these failure modes at once. "Fix" implies need or craving (CAP 18.7 "need (as opposed to merely enjoy)"; 18.6 "indispensable"). Its everyday and drug-slang meaning is a dose that resolves a lack, which is the "catalyst for a change in state" Diageo bans. It's also addressed to the individual, with no people and no occasion. Expect South African regulators and critics to read it the same way, though the separate SA researcher should confirm local code wording.
- High-risk social formats to avoid: Monday/Wednesday/"hump day"/Friday-countdown posts, "you've earned it" and "treat yourself", "self-care Saturday", "survival kit" bundles, Dry January jokes, before-and-after reels (flat then lit, or the non-alc drink shown dull), meme reposts about escaping reality, and "always got your back" friend-in-a-can copy. Each has an upheld ruling or regulator statement behind it.
- A "we meant friends" defence fails when the copy's structure is a problem followed by the drink (BrewDog, Jack Daniel's). The test is structural: if the post has a problem, a low point or an "until" in it, and the can resolves it, rewrite it.

### Gaps
- I found no verified case of a major global brand deleting a specific Instagram post after backlash for "quarantine" or "mummy juice" framing. The documented consequences are regulator rulings and NGO criticism, not confirmed takedowns by name.
- No ASA ruling was found that uses "you've earned it" or "you deserve it" verbatim. The closest are BrewDog (drink as solution) and Jack Daniel's (drinking time as the "good part"). This should be checked against SA ARA/ARB rulings by the SA researcher.
- ABAC Q2 2021 lists a complaint that an "Instagram post implies a significant change in mood". The brand and outcome weren't verified. — [ABAC Q2 2021 report](http://www.abac.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/ABAC-Q2-2021-Quarterly-Report-July-2021.pdf)

## 8. Repeatable patterns: distilled for a Doña Fuego do's and don'ts

### Takeaway
Across Heineken, Guinness, Diageo, Corona, Aperol and the tequila brands, the work that wins on effectiveness and stays clear of regulators follows one logic. The occasion is already good, the people make it, and the drink is what you bring and share. Ritual, place, food and humour about the occasion carry the content. Moderation and a zero-alcohol option show up as normal, attractive choices. Everything that fails puts the drink at the turning point of the story, where it solves a problem or lifts a mood.

### Inferences (draft do's and don'ts for Doña Fuego; these are my synthesis, not sourced claims)
**Do**
1. **Make the can the thing you bring, not the thing you need.** Use verbs of bringing and sharing: pack, bring, share, pass, sort the cooler box, "who's bringing the Fuego?" This is Cutwater's "bring the bar", Casamigos's "for friends" and the "share of cooler box" idea in practice.
2. **Start in an already-good moment.** The braai is lit, the umbrella is up, the friends are there. Fuego is in the cooler box, not the reason anyone came (Diageo §06h). There is no before-and-after.
3. **Put people, food and place in the frame first and the can second.** Tongs, boerewors, a beach towel, a Saturday-afternoon game of cricket. The product shot can be the clink or the cooler-box reveal (as in Heineken's clinking hands, Corona's lime, Aperol's serve).
4. **Own a small ritual.** The crack of the can, the clink, cans on ice, pouring over ice with a lime wedge, choosing between Margarita, Spicy and Paloma. Rituals are shareable without any mood claim (Guinness's pour, Corona's lime, Aperol's terrace serve).
5. **Make the hero message the product truth.** "Made with real tequila, lemon & lime juice" works like Patrón's "three ingredients". Make it about taste and what's in the can, never about what it does to you.
6. **Joke about the occasion and its enemies, not about drinking.** Phones at the braai, the friend whose "five minutes" is an hour (framed as a braai joke, not a "Fuego fixes it" setup), sunburn, the guy who forgets the ice. Make sure the can never resolves the problem (the BrewDog test).
7. **Show the zero-alcohol choice and water as equal and good-looking.** Someone on soda or water in the same frame, unremarked, never the punchline. Never imply a non-drinker is dull (Muff Liquor ruling; Diageo §02).
8. **Build in light-touch moderation the brand way.** A Guinness Clear-style nudge, such as "alternate with water" or "the driver gets first pick of the cooler box", designated-driver love, and the standard responsible tag and forward notice on shareable content.

**Don't**
1. **Don't use "fix", "need", "deserve", "earned it", "treat yourself", "self-care", "survival kit", "cure", "got your back", "rescue" or "pick-me-up".** Each frames the can as a remedy or reward (CAP 18.6 and 18.7; BrewDog, La Vieille Ferme, Reserve rulings).
2. **Don't run calendar-pain posts.** Avoid "Monday blues", "hump day", "Friday can't come soon enough", "skip to the good part" and "shut the laptop" (Captain Morgan and Jack Daniel's rulings; the Cuervo "Summer Fridays" edge case).
3. **Don't post before-and-after energy edits.** No flat party that pops when the cans arrive, and no dancing that gets wilder after the first sip (Muff Liquor).
4. **Don't post shots, downing or "finish it" challenges, and don't repost UGC drinking games.** Celebrate the ritual, not the speed (Diageo §04; how Guinness handled split the G).
5. **Don't promise confidence, attraction, "opening up" or popularity.** Even Heineken's "over a beer, we are more likely to open up" sits at the edge (Diageo §07; Heineken RMC).
6. **Don't joke about Dry January, sobriety or the person not drinking** ("One won't hurt, right?" ruling).
7. **Don't use loneliness, stress or bad-day hooks, even with sympathy.** Help people connect instead (Budweiser's check-in, Jägermeister's support for venues), and never offer the can as comfort.

**Rewrite example for the trigger story (inference):** "Get your Fuego fix" puts one person and a craving at the centre. Rewrites that keep it about bringing and sharing: "Cooler box: sorted." / "Who's bringing the Fuego?" / "Margarita, Spicy or Paloma: pick for the braai." / "Real tequila, real lemon & lime. Bring some for the table."

### Gaps
- None of the global sources measure whether "bring/share" copy beats "need/fix" copy on engagement for RTDs specifically. The effectiveness evidence (Worlds Apart, Guinness Lovely Days, Boring Phone) is brand-level and from beer and stout.
- South African specifics (ARA Code wording, ARB rulings, Checkers/Sixty60 retail context) are out of scope here and should come from the SA researcher's notes before the one-pager is finalised.
