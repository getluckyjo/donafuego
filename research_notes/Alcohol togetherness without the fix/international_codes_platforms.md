# International alcohol marketing codes and platform policies: where togetherness ends and "the fix" begins

Scope: best-practice benchmark from the UK (CAP/BCAP, ASA rulings, Portman Group), the US (DISCUS, Beer Institute), the EU (AVMSD), global producers (IARD, WFA), the WHO, and Meta, TikTok and Google/YouTube. South African rules are covered by a separate researcher. Verified as at 16 October 2026 unless stated. "Verbatim" means copied from the primary page fetched; secondary or paraphrased material is marked **[secondary]** or **[paraphrase]**.

Context for the reader: the trigger was an Instagram story for Doña Fuego (7% tequila RTD, cans, Checkers) reading "get your Fuego fix". The recurring test across every code below: alcohol may *accompany* a good time with people who are already having one; it may not *cause* the good time, *fix* a bad one, or be something you *need*.

## 1. UK CAP Code section 18 (non-broadcast) and BCAP Code section 19 (broadcast): verbatim rules and CAP guidance notes

### Takeaway
The UK codes are the strictest mature-market benchmark. CAP 18.3 says outright that drinking "may be portrayed as sociable" but must not be "a key component of the success" of a social event. CAP 18.6 and 18.7 rule out implying that alcohol is indispensable, overcomes boredom, loneliness or "other problems", has "therapeutic qualities", or can change mood. CAP's own guidance says marketers must not "imply that anyone might need (as opposed to merely enjoy) an alcoholic drink". That is the clearest written line against "fix" language.

### Cited Findings

**CAP Code section 18 (non-broadcast; covers brand social media, websites, influencer posts). Verbatim:**
- Principle: "Marketing communications for alcoholic drinks should not be targeted at people under 18 and should not imply, condone or encourage immoderate, irresponsible or anti-social drinking. The spirit as well as the letter of the rules applies." — [CAP Code s.18](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/18.html)
- Definition: the rules cover drinks "containing more than 0.5% alcohol" and also apply "where a soft drink is promoted as a mixer". — [CAP Code s.18](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/18.html)
- **18.1** "Marketing communications must be socially responsible and must contain nothing that is likely to lead people to adopt styles of drinking that are unwise. For example, they should not encourage excessive drinking. Care should be taken not to exploit the young, the immature or those who are mentally or socially vulnerable." — [CAP Code s.18](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/18.html)
- **18.2** "Marketing communications must not claim or imply that alcohol can enhance confidence or popularity." — [CAP Code s.18](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/18.html)
- **18.3** "Marketing communications must not imply that drinking alcohol is a key component of the success of a personal relationship or social event. The consumption of alcohol may be portrayed as sociable or thirst-quenching." — [CAP Code s.18](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/18.html)
- **18.4** "Drinking alcohol must not be portrayed as a challenge. Marketing communications must neither show, imply, encourage or refer to aggression or unruly, irresponsible or anti-social behaviour nor link alcohol with brave, tough or daring people or behaviour." — [CAP Code s.18](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/18.html)
- **18.5** "Marketing communications must neither link alcohol with seduction, sexual activity or sexual success nor imply that alcohol can enhance attractiveness." — [CAP Code s.18](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/18.html)
- **18.6** "Marketing communications must not imply that alcohol might be indispensable or take priority in life or that drinking alcohol can overcome boredom, loneliness or other problems." — [CAP Code s.18](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/18.html)
- **18.7** "Marketing communications must not imply that alcohol has therapeutic qualities. Alcohol must not be portrayed as capable of changing mood, physical condition or behaviour or as a source of nourishment. Marketing communications must not imply that alcohol can enhance mental or physical capabilities; for example, by contributing to professional or sporting achievements." — [CAP Code s.18](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/18.html)
- **18.9** (strength, relevant to a 7% RTD): "Marketing communications must not imply that a drink may be preferred because of its alcohol content or intoxicating effect… In the case of a drink with relatively high alcoholic strength in relation to its category, the factual information should not be given undue emphasis." — [CAP Code s.18](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/18.html)
- **18.10** "Marketing communications that include a promotion must not imply, condone or encourage excessive consumption of alcohol." — [CAP Code s.18](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/18.html)
- **18.14** "Marketing communications must not be likely to appeal particularly to people under 18, especially by reflecting or being associated with youth culture… People shown drinking or playing a significant role (see rule 18.16) should not be shown behaving in an adolescent or juvenile manner." — [CAP Code s.18](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/18.html)
- **18.15** "…No medium should be used to advertise alcoholic drinks if more than 25% of its audience is under 18 years of age." — [CAP Code s.18](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/18.html)
- **18.16** "People shown drinking or playing a significant role must neither be nor seem to be under 25. People under 25 may be shown in marketing communications, for example, in the context of family celebrations, but must be obviously not drinking." — [CAP Code s.18](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/18.html)
- **18.17** "…must not make any health, fitness or weight-control claims." — [CAP Code s.18](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/18.html)

**BCAP Code section 19 (TV and radio). Verbatim. Several rules are worded more explicitly than CAP:**
- **19.2** "Advertisements must not feature, imply, condone or encourage irresponsible or immoderate drinking. That applies to both the amount of drink and the way drinking is portrayed." It also bars "References to, or suggestions of, buying repeat rounds". — [BCAP Code s.19](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/broadcast/code_section/19.html)
- **19.3** "Advertisements must neither imply that alcohol can contribute to an individual's popularity or confidence nor imply that alcohol can enhance personal qualities" — [BCAP Code s.19](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/broadcast/code_section/19.html)
- **19.4** "Advertisements must not imply that drinking alcohol is a key component of social success or acceptance or that refusal is a sign of weakness. Advertisements must not imply that the success of a social occasion depends on the presence or consumption of alcohol." — [BCAP Code s.19](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/broadcast/code_section/19.html)
- **19.5** "Advertisements must not link alcohol with daring, toughness, aggression or unruly, irresponsible or antisocial behaviour." — [BCAP Code s.19](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/broadcast/code_section/19.html)
- **19.6** "…That does not preclude linking alcohol with romance or flirtation." — [BCAP Code s.19](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/broadcast/code_section/19.html)
- **19.7** "Advertisements must not portray alcohol as indispensable or as taking priority in life. Advertisements must not imply that drinking can overcome problems or that regular solitary drinking is acceptable" — [BCAP Code s.19](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/broadcast/code_section/19.html)
- **19.8** "Advertisements must not imply that alcohol has therapeutic qualities. Alcohol must not be portrayed as capable of changing mood, physical condition or behaviour or as a source of nourishment. Although they may refer to refreshment, advertisements must not imply that alcohol can improve any type of performance." — [BCAP Code s.19](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/broadcast/code_section/19.html)
- **19.17** "Alcohol advertisements must not feature in a significant role anyone who is, or seems to be, under 25 and must not feature children." There is an exception for "families socialising responsibly". — [BCAP Code s.19](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/broadcast/code_section/19.html)

**CAP Advice Online guidance notes. Verbatim from the CAP Executive; non-binding:**
- *Alcohol: Social events* (08 Jan 2021): "Although drinkers may obviously be shown in a group setting and alcohol can be conveyed as a legitimate accompaniment to a social occasion, marketers should not unduly emphasise the role alcohol might play. If an otherwise dull party becomes a social triumph as a result of alcohol, the ad will almost certainly breach the Code." — [CAP Advice: Alcohol: Social events](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/alcohol-social-events.html)
- Same note: "Whether or not something breaches the Code in this way can often be a fine line". — [CAP Advice: Alcohol: Social events](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/alcohol-social-events.html)
- *Alcohol: Therapeutic claims* (updated 20 May 2026). On stimulant/sedative claims: "Because marketers should not suggest that alcohol is a stimulant; claims such as 'energising' are likely to be unacceptable… The Code also prohibits implying that alcohol can be a sedative, so references to an alcoholic product being a tranquilliser, or in some other way calming, are problematic." — [CAP Advice: Therapeutic claims](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/alcohol-therapeutic-claims.html)
- Same note, on relaxation (the key "after a hard day" passage): "Addressing or showing people who are in a 'normal' state of balance and suggesting alcohol can help them relax may be acceptable – however, marketers should be very wary of these types of claims, as these implications could easily cross the line into unacceptability. Additionally, featuring or encouraging those who are clearly stressed or more anxious than normal to drink alcohol is likely to be interpreted as saying that alcohol is necessary for relaxation, has therapeutic qualities or mental health benefits – all of which are likely to breach rule 18.7". — [CAP Advice: Therapeutic claims](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/alcohol-therapeutic-claims.html)
- Same note, on "chill": "Claims such as 'chill with your mates' may be acceptable depending on context – however, marketers should bear in mind that, if the ad implies alcohol can make drinkers more 'chilled', the claim could be the wrong side of the line." — [CAP Advice: Therapeutic claims](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/alcohol-therapeutic-claims.html)
- Same note, on mental health: "Alcohol marketers must not imply that consuming alcohol can improve one's mental health, suggest that alcohol is necessary for good mental health, or suggest that alcohol can provide emotional support." — [CAP Advice: Therapeutic claims](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/alcohol-therapeutic-claims.html)
- Same note, on **need versus enjoy** (closest written line to "fix"): "Marketers must remember that they should not imply or state that alcohol has any health benefits, promote alcohol on a health platform, or imply that anyone might need (as opposed to merely enjoy) an alcoholic drink." — [CAP Advice: Therapeutic claims](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/alcohol-therapeutic-claims.html)
- Same note, on humour: "Marketers should also be aware that humour does not invalidate the need to comply with the Code." — [CAP Advice: Therapeutic claims](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/alcohol-therapeutic-claims.html)
- *Alcohol: Boredom and loneliness* (18 Jul 2024): "Showing a depressed person sitting with a bottle of booze in one hand and a glass in the other is obviously unacceptable, as are ads that irresponsibly emphasise the importance of alcohol, or suggest that it can be used as a means of dealing with difficult, stressful or boring situations." — [CAP Advice: Boredom and loneliness](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/alcohol-boredom-and-loneliness.html)
- Same note, on Dry January: such references are "not always or inherently problematic, in many cases it will be, since the intended effect from alcohol marketers will often be that Dry January or sobriety is difficult or boring." — [CAP Advice: Boredom and loneliness](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/alcohol-boredom-and-loneliness.html)
- *Alcohol: Challenges, bravery and machismo* (05 Aug 2026): "Asking whether drinkers 'can handle it' or are 'up for it'; including warnings about the product's strength; using macho-type images; or suggesting that an alcohol product could contribute to enhancing personal confidence, are likely to render ads a problem. Marketers should also take care not to imply that refusing drinks or drinking responsibly is a sign of weakness". — [CAP Advice: Challenges, bravery and machismo](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/alcohol-challenges-bravery-and-machismo.html)
- *Alcohol: General* (25 Nov 2024) states the rules "apply irrespective of whether the product is shown or seen being consumed". It adds: "Given the socio-political climate in which alcohol marketers now operate, the Copy Advice team tends to take a cautious approach when giving advice on alcohol ads." — [CAP Advice: Alcohol: General](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/alcohol-general.html)

### Inferences
- "Get your Fuego fix" engages CAP 18.6 and 18.7 together. "Fix" reads as something you need, crave or are hooked on, which falls under "indispensable" and "need (as opposed to merely enjoy)". It also reads as remedying a deficit, which falls under "overcome… other problems" and "therapeutic qualities". No ASA ruling was found on the word "fix" itself (see Gaps), so this is a reasoned application of the wording, not a precedent.
- The permitted space is explicit and narrow. "Sociable", "thirst-quenching", "refreshment" (BCAP 19.8), "romance or flirtation" (BCAP 19.6), "legitimate accompaniment to a social occasion", and people who are already happy and already together.
- The UK places relaxation in a grey zone. CAP allows "relax" for people in a "normal" state of balance but warns it "could easily cross the line". Any stressed or "hard day" framing is likely a breach. Portman (section 3) is stricter, so a cautious brand should avoid "relax"-from-the-drink claims altogether.

### Gaps
- I did not find a CAP Advice Online note titled "therapeutic/mood" separate from "Therapeutic claims". The "mood" guidance sits inside that note and the "Boredom and loneliness" note.
- I did not separately fetch the CAP notes on "Confidence and popularity" or "Sex and personal relationships". Confidence guidance appears inside the "Challenges, bravery and machismo" note.

## 2. UK ASA rulings applying these rules to social media and digital posts (coping, relaxing, surviving, boredom, deserving, courage, the drink making the party)

### Takeaway
The ASA consistently upholds complaints where a drink is cast as the answer to a bad day, a boring week, low mood, stress, "tough times" or Dry January, including when the post is jokey, a meme or an influencer's. Togetherness campaigns pass where people are already happy and bonded and the drink is incidental.

### Cited Findings

**Upheld: drink as coping, mood fix or emotional support**
- **Perrin et Fils SAS t/a La Vieille Ferme (wine), TikTok, ruling 20 May 2026.** Three boxed-wine cartons were captioned "One to resurrect my soul", "One for emotional support" and "One to erase today", with mental-health copy including "Emotional support is vital for our mental health…". The ASA "concluded that the ad implied that drinking alcohol could enhance one's mood and emotional well-being, which implied that alcohol had therapeutic qualities." Upheld (18.7). — [CAP Advice: Therapeutic claims](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/alcohol-therapeutic-claims.html); ruling: [ASA ruling, Perrin et Fils](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/perrin-et-fils-sas-g26-1334485-perrin-et-fils-sas.html)
- **Brewdog plc ("Wingman" beer), poster, ruling 6 August 2025.** Copy: "BREWDOG. ALWAYS GOT YOUR BACK… Some things in life go AWOL – WiFi fails, the weather turns hostile, and your buddy's 'five minutes' turns into a full-scale delay. But Wingman? Wingman stands firm…". The ASA said that although the language was metaphorical, "the scenarios presented were real-life problems that were likely to impact people in a negative way, and the beer was a solution to overcoming those problems." Upheld. — [CAP Advice: Therapeutic claims](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/alcohol-therapeutic-claims.html); ruling: [ASA ruling, Brewdog](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/brewdog-plc-a25-1291535-brewdog-plc.html)
- **Blackrose Ltd (prosecco), Facebook post, ruling 11 December 2019.** Copy: "If at first you don't succeed, try drinking a bottle of prosecco, you'll be surprised at how much less you'll care". The post was acknowledged as parody, but "as the ad suggested that prosecco could help one forget their problems, it breached rule 18.7". — [CAP Advice: Therapeutic claims](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/alcohol-therapeutic-claims.html); ruling: [ASA ruling, Blackrose](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/blackrose-ltd-A19-1034241.html)
- **Drake & Morgan Ltd (bar group), online quiz, ruling 9 October 2019.** The quiz offered a voucher for a "potion to match their emotion", asked users whether they were "overstretched", "low-spirited" or "walking on sunshine", and used "give yourself a boost". Found in breach. — [CAP Advice: Therapeutic claims](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/alcohol-therapeutic-claims.html); ruling: [ASA ruling, Drake & Morgan](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/drake-morgan-ltd-G19-1020179.html)
- **Muff Liquor Company / influencer Laura Whitmore, Instagram and TikTok video (July 2022), reported March 2023.** Caption: "If drinks were dance moves @muffliquorco #makemineamuff". The ASA found she danced "more confidently and enthusiastically" when drinking Muff and Tonic than with soft drinks, "implying to consumers that drinking Muff and Tonic would enhance their confidence and improve their mood". The TikTok version also breached 18.15 (audience) because of her Love Island audience. — **[secondary]** [RPC Legal summary](https://www.rpclegal.com/snapshots/advertising-and-marketing/spring-2023/asa-slams-social-media-post-for-breaching-rules-on-alcohol-advertising/)
- **Novielo London, "PRESCRIPTION ALCOHOL" wine-label sticker, ruling 7 June 2023.** Copy included "DOSE: Drink one 175ml dose twice daily. If symptoms persist, take double the dose stated…". The ASA found that although customers would know it was not a real prescription, "it linked alcohol to medicine and implied alcohol could be used to maintain good health". — [CAP Advice: Therapeutic claims](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/alcohol-therapeutic-claims.html)
- **Wild Cape Liqueurs (Wild Africa Cream), ruling 1 July 2009.** The strapline "Unleash your wild side" "suggested that Wild Africa Cream Liqueur had changed a woman's mood and enhanced her confidence". Upheld. — [CAP Advice: Therapeutic claims](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/alcohol-therapeutic-claims.html)
- **2000 ruling (advertiser not named in the note).** "Vodka rush" was upheld because it "implied alcohol could increase drinkers' energy levels and act as a stimulant." — [CAP Advice: Therapeutic claims](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/alcohol-therapeutic-claims.html)

**Upheld: "survive the week", boredom, Dry January, the drink as the good part of the day**
- **Reserve Ltd (wine retailer), email, ruling 10 March 2021.** Subject "Friend, forget Dry January". The body included "January's always a tough month…" and "we thought you might need all the help you could get over the next few weeks, so we've put together Lockdown 3.0 - a 6-bottle mixed case… A survival kit, if you will." The ASA found the overall impression "was that alcohol could overcome problems, including boredom and low mood". This is the closest precedent found for "survive the week" or survival-kit framing. — [CAP Advice: Boredom and loneliness](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/alcohol-boredom-and-loneliness.html); ruling: [ASA ruling, Reserve](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/reserve-ltd-A21-1092471.html)
- **Brown-Forman (Jack Daniel's), ruling 28 February 2024.** Copy: "Shorter days mean we can skip to the good part". The advertiser said the ad was about time with friends. The ASA "felt that the ad implied that the rest of the day was tedious and to be endured until drinking time began". Breach of 18.6. This is the closest precedent found for "wine o'clock" or "finally Friday" framing. — [CAP Advice: Boredom and loneliness](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/alcohol-boredom-and-loneliness.html); ruling: [ASA ruling, Brown-Forman](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/brown-forman-beverages-europe-ltd-a23-1223370-brown-forman-beverages-europe-ltd.html). Medium not confirmed in the sources fetched.
- **Diageo Great Britain Ltd, ruling 23 July 2014.** A pirate raising a glass with "Wednesday. I'm declaring war on mid-week boredom" was "problematic for suggesting that drinking could alleviate boredom". This is the closest precedent found for "beat the Monday blues" or "hump-day" framing. — [CAP Advice: Boredom and loneliness](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/alcohol-boredom-and-loneliness.html); ruling: [ASA ruling, Diageo 2014](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/Diageo-Great-Britain-Ltd-A14-270730.html)
- **Global Brands Ltd (VK Blue), paid Instagram story, April 2025.** Copy: "Doing Dry January? One won't hurt, right? And yes, the 70cl bottles totally count! Pick up a 70cl bottle of VK Blue at B&M for just £1.99 and keep the party going into January!" Breach of 18.1 and 18.6. The ASA said "the fundamental purpose of the ad was to tempt consumers taking part in Dry January to drink alcohol". — **[secondary]** [The Drinks Business, 22 May 2025](https://www.thedrinksbusiness.com/2025/05/alcohol-brands-under-fire-for-irresponsible-social-media-ads/)
- **Drink Doctor Ltd ("Crateman"), meme posts, ruling 24 May 2017.** Memes included "People are not addicted to alcohol or drugs, they are addicted to escaping reality" and "I see people all the time going to therapy, thinking they've got a problem… order a Crateman and live a little...or a lot". The advertiser said it had only attached its brand to popular memes. The ASA held that the ads implied "alcohol could overcome boredom, loneliness and other personal problems". — [CAP Advice: Boredom and loneliness](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/alcohol-boredom-and-loneliness.html); ruling: [ASA ruling, Drink Doctor](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/drink-doctor-ltd-a17-368781.html)

**Upheld: indispensable or priority in life (the "need" axis)**
- **Hi-Spirits Ltd (Fireball cinnamon whisky), ruling 17 July 2013.** Copy: "I'd dump my boyfriend for more Fireball cinnamon whisky" and "The only thing in life more important to me than Fireball is…". The ASA rejected the defence that these were jokes. Breach of 18.6 (indispensable / priority in life). — [CAP Advice: Boredom and loneliness](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/alcohol-boredom-and-loneliness.html); ruling: [ASA ruling, Hi-Spirits](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/Hi-Spirits-Ltd-A13-228670.html)

**Upheld: courage, toughness, daring (the "liquid courage" axis)**
- **Wells & Young Brewing ("Courage" beer), ruling 15 April 2009.** "Take Courage, my friend" was unacceptable because, "although light-hearted, it implied that alcohol could provide the drinker with Dutch courage, confidence or comfort". — [CAP Advice: Challenges, bravery and machismo](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/alcohol-challenges-bravery-and-machismo.html)
- **AU Vodka Ltd ("The Vodfather"), Instagram, ruling 23 August 2023.** Gangster characters "linked alcohol with tough, daring and aggressive behaviour". The ASA accepted the ads were meant to be comic and upheld anyway. — [CAP Advice: Challenges, bravery and machismo](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/alcohol-challenges-bravery-and-machismo.html); ruling: [ASA ruling, AU Vodka](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/au-vodka-ltd-a23-1198294-au-vodka-ltd.html)
- **DrunkH Ltd (drinking card game), online ads, ruling 14 December 2022.** Copy: "Warning: This game is NOT for snowflakes", "UK's most irresponsible drinking game", "Not for the faint of heart". The ads portrayed "drinking as a challenge". — [CAP Advice: Challenges, bravery and machismo](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/alcohol-challenges-bravery-and-machismo.html)
- **Edrington (The Macallan), ruling 10 April 2019.** "Would you risk falling ... for the chance to fly?" was a fantastical scenario but still promoted "risky or daring behaviour". — [CAP Advice: Challenges, bravery and machismo](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/alcohol-challenges-bravery-and-machismo.html)

**Upheld: the drink makes the party or the moment**
- **Freixenet UK Ltd, brand website, ruling 11 May 2016.** Copy: "Moments that might be made more fun with a bottle of Freixenet fizz" and "Somehow any moment is made just that bit more fun, just that bit more exciting and crazy, when it's accompanied with our bubbles". Upheld because "the alcohol was presented as essential, rather than a legitimate accompaniment to occasions". — [CAP Advice: Social events](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/alcohol-social-events.html); ruling: [ASA ruling, Freixenet](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/freixenet-uk-ltd-a15-316701.html)
- **Bacardi-Martini Ltd, cinema ad, 19 January 2005.** A station-platform party ad was irresponsible "because alcohol consumption was portrayed as the main reason for the party and for its success". — [CAP Advice: Social events](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/alcohol-social-events.html)

**Upheld: excessive or rapid drinking on Instagram, and age appeal despite 18+ targeting**
- **Phusion Projects LLC (Four Loko), Instagram, ruling 21 May 2025.** The image and "SEND IT" with three rocket emojis were read as urging rapid consumption. Breach of 18.1. — **[secondary]** [The Drinks Business](https://www.thedrinksbusiness.com/2025/05/alcohol-brands-under-fire-for-irresponsible-social-media-ads/)
- **Bestway Retail (Bargain Booze), Facebook and Instagram Santa video, ruling 19 March 2025.** Breach of 18.14 (particular appeal to under-18s) "Despite targeting the ad at users aged 18 and over". The ASA noted that Facebook and Instagram "do not enforce robust age-verification systems". — **[secondary]** [The Drinks Business](https://www.thedrinksbusiness.com/2025/05/alcohol-brands-under-fire-for-irresponsible-social-media-ads/)

**Not upheld: the togetherness benchmarks (what passes)**
- **Coors Brewers (Carling "Belong"), 7 May 2008.** Starlings flocking and "YOU KNOW WHO YOUR MATES ARE". The ASA held the ads "would be viewed as referring to a spirit of togetherness rather than suggesting that alcohol was a reason for the success of any personal relationship or social event". Not upheld. — [CAP Advice: Social events](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/alcohol-social-events.html)
- **Diageo Great Britain, YouTube video, 19 October 2016.** People getting ready for a night out, with "bring wherever you're from", "bring your light", "bring moves". Not upheld: "all characters seemed happy and excited before their events, and the slogans were likely to be interpreted as a reference to the characters bringing 'themselves' (their personalities) to the event". — [CAP Advice: Social events](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/alcohol-social-events.html); ruling: [ASA ruling, Diageo 2016](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/diageo-great-britain-ltd-a16-346175.html)
- **Bacardi Global Brands (Bombay Sapphire artists), 8 January 2020.** Not upheld because the artists were "already creatively minded" and "not shown consuming alcohol before, during or after their work". — [CAP Advice: Therapeutic claims](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/alcohol-therapeutic-claims.html)

### Inferences
- Across these rulings, the ASA asks one question: **what state are people in before the drink, and what changes because of it?** If people start happy and together and the drink is incidental, the ad passes (Carling, Diageo 2016). If people start stressed, bored, sad or stuck and the drink is the turning point, or the drink is "the good part", "a survival kit" or "emotional support", the ad is upheld.
- Humour, memes, parody, metaphor and influencer delivery are not defences (Blackrose, Drink Doctor, Brewdog, Fireball, Whitmore).
- "Survive the week", "Monday blues", "wine o'clock" and "you deserve it after the day you've had" are not tested by exact phrase in what I found. Reserve ("survival kit"), Diageo 2014 ("mid-week boredom"), Jack Daniel's ("skip to the good part") and La Vieille Ferme ("erase today") are close enough analogues that a UK regulator would very likely treat them the same way.
- The Bargain Booze ruling matters for Instagram stories. Even 18+ targeting on Meta is not treated as robust age assurance, so content must also avoid youth appeal.

### Gaps
- I found no ASA ruling quoting "wine o'clock", "you deserve it", "self-care", "liquid courage" (as opposed to "Take Courage"/"Dutch courage") or "fix". Searches on asa.org.uk did not surface them. It is unconfirmed whether such rulings exist.
- The La Vieille Ferme (2026), Brewdog (2025), Blackrose, Reserve, Diageo and Jack Daniel's details come from the CAP Advice Online summaries, not the full ruling texts. The full rulings were not fetched, so exact rule numbers cited in each ruling and any "Action" wording are not quoted.
- The VK, Four Loko, Bargain Booze and Whitmore/Muff details come from trade or legal press summaries **[secondary]**.

## 3. Portman Group Code of Practice (6th edition, amended), Independent Complaints Panel decisions, and guidance on sociability and mood

### Takeaway
Portman's 6th edition (amended; complaints from 20 February 2024) bans suggesting that a drink leads to "social success or popularity" (3.2(e)) or can "change mood or behaviour" (3.2(j)). Its guidance is the most explicit statement anywhere of what *is* acceptable: showing a drink "in a happy, group setting" and as "a legitimate accompaniment to a sociable and relaxing setting". It is equally explicit about what is not: "Do not claim that anyone needs, as opposed to likes, or enjoys, a drink"; "Icebreaker", "Social Lubricator" and "Dutch Courage"; and any suggestion that the drink "helped the consumer relax".

### Cited Findings

**Code text, verbatim (Sixth Edition, Amended):**
- Scope: the Code applies to "the naming, packaging, marketing and promotional activity undertaken by a drink's producer… where such activity is primarily UK-targeted, and is not already subject to regulation through the ASA or Ofcom" (1.3). It is "to be applied in the spirit as well as in the letter" (1.6). "Complaints received on or after 20 February 2024 will be considered under this Code" (1.11). — [Portman NPP Code, 6th ed. (amended), PDF](https://portmangroup21.wpenginepowered.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Portman-Group-NPP-Sixth-Edition-Amended.pdf)
- **3.2** "A drink, its packaging and any promotional material or activity should not in any direct or indirect way:" — [Portman NPP Code](https://portmangroup21.wpenginepowered.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Portman-Group-NPP-Sixth-Edition-Amended.pdf)
  - "(a) give the higher alcoholic strength, or intoxicating effect, undue emphasis…"
  - "(b) suggest any association with bravado, or with violent, aggressive, dangerous, anti-social or illegal behaviour…"
  - "(d) suggest any association with sexual activity or sexual success;"
  - "(e) suggest that consumption of the drink can lead to social success or popularity;"
  - "(f) encourage illegal, irresponsible or immoderate consumption, such as drink-driving, binge-drinking or drunkenness;"
  - "(g) urge the consumer to drink rapidly or to 'down' a product in one;"
  - "(h) have a particular appeal to under-18s…"
  - "(i) incorporate images of people who are, or look as if they are, under 25 years of age, where there is any suggestion that they are drinking alcohol, or they are featured in a significant role…"
  - "(j) suggest that the product has therapeutic qualities, can enhance mental or physical capabilities, or change mood or behaviour."
- **3.3** "A drink's name, its packaging and any promotional material or activity should not cause serious or widespread offence." — [Portman NPP Code](https://portmangroup21.wpenginepowered.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Portman-Group-NPP-Sixth-Edition-Amended.pdf)
- Portman says the 2019 sixth-edition revision introduced "the requirement that… marketing cannot imply that alcohol can change mood or behaviour", plus "new guidance on immoderate consumption". — [Portman Codes of Practice page](https://www.portmangroup.org.uk/codes-of-practice/)

**Guidance Note, Rule 3.2(e) Social success or popularity (March 2019). Verbatim:**
- "While it is perfectly permissible to show people consuming a drink to be sociable and popular, there should be no suggestion that consumption of the drink has played a part in an individual achieving popularity or sociability. Likewise, alcohol should not be presented as the catalyst for the success of an event." — [Portman Guidance 3.2(e)](https://www.portmangroup.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/Guidance-Note-Rule-3.2e-Social-success-or-popularity.pdf)
- "Marketers should avoid any implication that alcohol can enhance a person's confidence, help them overcome shyness, or be essential to them having a good time. Expressions such as 'Icebreaker', 'Social Lubricator' or 'Dutch Courage' will be unacceptable." — [Portman Guidance 3.2(e)](https://www.portmangroup.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/Guidance-Note-Rule-3.2e-Social-success-or-popularity.pdf)
- "It should not be suggested that alcohol has played a key part in the success of an event, for example, by suggesting that the presence or consumption of alcohol can transform a dull/boring occasion into a more lively/successful event. It should not be suggested that a person is in any way 'losing out' for not being part of an alcohol-related scene." — [Portman Guidance 3.2(e)](https://www.portmangroup.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/Guidance-Note-Rule-3.2e-Social-success-or-popularity.pdf)
- **What is acceptable:** "There is not necessarily a problem with suggesting that people who drink the product are sociable and popular, for example by showing or alluding to consumption of the drink in a happy, group setting." And: "It is acceptable to portray drinking as a legitimate accompaniment to a sociable and relaxing setting, even among a group of people who appear to be confident and/or attractive." — [Portman Guidance 3.2(e)](https://www.portmangroup.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/Guidance-Note-Rule-3.2e-Social-success-or-popularity.pdf)
- **Decision, WKD (2008):** consumer photos on the WKD "Mugshots" website. The Panel found that "the effect was to imply close bonding, popularity, and a good time, all very closely linked to the WKD brand… some of the photographs… went further and suggested that consumption of WKD could lead to social success and popularity". Breach of 3.2(e). — [Portman Guidance 3.2(e)](https://www.portmangroup.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/Guidance-Note-Rule-3.2e-Social-success-or-popularity.pdf)
- **Decision, Rampant TT's (2009):** event photos on the brand website, including a sign reading "I'm off my tits". The Panel considered that they "implied that consumption of Rampant would lead to social success or popularity". The company had removed the images on notification and said it had not produced the signs. — [Portman Guidance 3.2(e)](https://www.portmangroup.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/Guidance-Note-Rule-3.2e-Social-success-or-popularity.pdf)

**Guidance Note, Rule 3.2(j) Therapeutic qualities… change in mood or behaviour. Verbatim:**
- "Any direct or implied suggestion that consumption of alcohol has helped the consumer relax is likely to be unacceptable under the Code." — [Portman Guidance 3.2(j)](https://www.portmangroup.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/guidance-on-rule-3-2j-therapeutic-qualities.pdf)
- "Alcohol must not be the catalyst for change of mood or mental state." — [Portman Guidance 3.2(j)](https://www.portmangroup.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/guidance-on-rule-3-2j-therapeutic-qualities.pdf)
- "The portrayal of drinking as a legitimate accompaniment to a relaxing setting will be acceptable. For example, expressions where the relaxation element is attributed to one's surroundings/setting, and not the alcohol, which is a by-product of the setting, are likely to be acceptable. However, any claims that directly link consumption with relaxation will be unacceptable." — [Portman Guidance 3.2(j)](https://www.portmangroup.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/guidance-on-rule-3-2j-therapeutic-qualities.pdf)
- **"Do not claim that anyone needs, as opposed to likes, or enjoys, a drink."** — [Portman Guidance 3.2(j)](https://www.portmangroup.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/guidance-on-rule-3-2j-therapeutic-qualities.pdf)
- "Claims that alcohol can make the drinker smarter, wittier, sharper, more focused, stronger, faster or higher performing in some way are likely to be unacceptable." — [Portman Guidance 3.2(j)](https://www.portmangroup.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/guidance-on-rule-3-2j-therapeutic-qualities.pdf)
- "…those activities which suggest that they can help consumers to 'forget' their everyday lives and see things through the rose-tinted lens of an alcoholic drink are likely to be problematic." — [Portman Guidance 3.2(j)](https://www.portmangroup.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/guidance-on-rule-3-2j-therapeutic-qualities.pdf)
- "References to the taste and quality of a product are likely to be fine under the Code and the Advisory Service would encourage producers to focus on these elements when making emotive statements. However, suggestions that a drink can invoke feelings/emotions, directly caused by consumption of the product, for example 'get in touch with your wild side', are likely to be problematic." — [Portman Guidance 3.2(j)](https://www.portmangroup.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/guidance-on-rule-3-2j-therapeutic-qualities.pdf)
- On energy and stimulants: "Alcohol is a depressant and it is therefore irresponsible and misleading to market an alcoholic drink in a way which implies it will make the consumer feel more energetic". — [Portman Guidance 3.2(j)](https://www.portmangroup.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/guidance-on-rule-3-2j-therapeutic-qualities.pdf)
- **Decision, Dr Von Hyde's Herbal Liqueur (2012):** promotional claims "cure for all", "a healer" and "medication for the masses" were found to claim medicinal properties. Breach. — [Portman Guidance 3.2(j)](https://www.portmangroup.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/guidance-on-rule-3-2j-therapeutic-qualities.pdf)
- **Decision, Vitesse Noir (2012):** packaging lines "this beer is so packed full of beans it could have you dancing on the ceiling" and "just when you are not sure you can manage the last dance, take a nip and it'll be just the tonic that you needed" were upheld for implying enhanced physical capability. — [Portman Guidance 3.2(j)](https://www.portmangroup.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/guidance-on-rule-3-2j-therapeutic-qualities.pdf)

**Recent ICP decision on mood (product copy):**
- **Engine Organic Gin (Engine S.r.l / distributed by Disaronno), 27 April 2023.** The back label read "sage and lemon is a traditional remedy to cure a sour mood", and the front read "fuel the dream". Upheld under 3.2(j). The Panel "was concerned the suggestion of a 'cure' could directly appeal to those with poor mental health who may be more susceptible to substance misuse." ICP Chair Nicola Williams: "Linking alcohol with improving a consumer's mood is a clear infringement of the Code and alcohol producers should not suggest their drink has therapeutic qualities." Not upheld under 3.2(e), since there was nothing suggesting popularity or social success. — [Portman press release, Engine Organic Gin](https://www.portmangroup.org.uk/complaint-against-engine-organic-gin-upheld-for-suggestion-of-therapeutic-qualities/)
- Portman's 2023 round-up: "Five complaints were also upheld under Code rule 3.2(j)… and five under 3.2(f)" (immoderate consumption). — [Portman, upheld complaints 2023](https://www.portmangroup.org.uk/upheld-complaints-more-than-double-in-2023-with-rise-in-alcohol-products-found-to-have-particular-appeal-to-under-18s/)

**Division of labour with the ASA:**
- CAP's general alcohol note says: "Whilst the ASA cannot comment on the packaging and names of alcoholic drinks, the Portman Group… should be able to do so." — [CAP Advice: Alcohol: General](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/alcohol-general.html)
- Portman's footnote 9: "The CAP non-broadcast Code covers advertisements and other marketing communications by or from companies… on their own websites, or in other non-paid-for space online under their control". — [Portman NPP Code](https://portmangroup21.wpenginepowered.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Portman-Group-NPP-Sixth-Edition-Amended.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a brand Instagram story in the UK, the ASA (CAP) would be the regulator, not Portman. Portman's rules matter for the **product name, can design, point-of-sale and sampling**. A strapline like "your Fuego fix" printed on a can or on a Checkers shelf wobbler would be the Portman analogue.
- Portman's "needs, as opposed to likes, or enjoys" is the single most quotable line for the one-pager. "Fix" is, semantically, a need word.
- Engine Gin shows that one mood-"cure" phrase on otherwise compliant packaging is enough to breach. It also shows that panels weigh the risk to people with poor mental health, which is the vulnerability the "fix" framing invokes.
- Portman's acceptable-setting formula ("relaxation… attributed to one's surroundings/setting, and not the alcohol") gives a usable copy rule. Credit the braai, the sunset, the mates or the long weekend, never the can.

### Gaps
- I did not find a stand-alone current Portman guidance note on **social media**. The Portman-run codes primarily govern packaging, naming, POS, sponsorship and PR. Brand-controlled social posts fall under the CAP Code. A 2000s-era Portman "digital marketing guide" is referenced by [Osborne Clarke](https://marketinglaw.osborneclarke.com/retailing/new-portman-group-guide-to-digital-marketing-of-alcohol/) but was not fetched.
- Product names of the other four 2023 3.2(j) upheld cases were not retrieved.
- The 3.2(j) guidance note in the fetched PDF predates panel precedent on the mood limb ("there are no precedents…"). An updated version reflecting Engine Gin was not located.

## 4. US codes: DISCUS Code of Responsible Practices and Beer Institute Advertising and Marketing Code (content rules, digital guidelines, audience thresholds)

### Takeaway
The US codes prohibit claims that alcohol brings "social, professional, educational, or athletic success or status" and "curative or therapeutic claims". They are narrower than the UK on mood: the Beer Institute only bars claims that people "cannot" succeed or "cannot solve social, personal, or physical problems without beer". Their real value is the digital mechanics. Both use a 73.8% legal-drinking-age (21+) audience threshold, and both require age affirmation, UGC moderation, forward-advice notices, 25+ models and influencer age-gating.

### Cited Findings

**DISCUS Code of Responsible Practices ("Last Update: July 2025"). Verbatim:**
- Edition: "Last Update: July 2025". — [DISCUS Code PDF (2025)](https://distilledspirits.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Final-2025-DISCUS-Code.pdf); landing page: [DISCUS Code of Responsible Practices](https://distilledspirits.org/code-of-responsible-practices/)
- **2B.3.b Irresponsible Content.** Violations "include, but are not limited to" materials that contain:
  - "ii. Portrayals of persons in a state of intoxication or suggesting intoxication is socially acceptable conduct, or promoting the intoxicating effects of beverage alcohol consumption."
  - "iv. Claims or representations that individuals can attain social, professional, educational, or athletic success or status due to beverage alcohol consumption."
  - "v. Promotions of alcohol as a 'rite of passage' to adulthood."
  - "viii. Curative or therapeutic claims related to a beverage alcohol product, except as permitted by law."
  - "x. Promotions of the potency of a beverage alcohol product…"
  — [DISCUS Code PDF (2025)](https://distilledspirits.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Final-2025-DISCUS-Code.pdf)
- **2B.3.a Affirmative Responsibility Statement:** "responsible drinking statements should be included in beverage alcohol advertising, marketing materials, and promotional events where practicable." — [DISCUS Code PDF](https://distilledspirits.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Final-2025-DISCUS-Code.pdf)
- **2B.2 Age of Models and Actors:** "models and actors employed should be a minimum of 25 years old, substantiated by proper identification, and should reasonably appear to be 21 years of age and older." Recognisable influencers of legal age are carved out but "should reasonably appear to be 21 years of age or older… and should not primarily appeal to persons below the legal purchase age." — [DISCUS Code PDF](https://distilledspirits.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Final-2025-DISCUS-Code.pdf)
- **2A.1 Adult Audiences (placement):** placement "where at least 73.8 percent of the audience is reasonably expected to be 21 or older, or where a platform/medium has been utilized to directly communicate to or target an audience of legal purchase age individuals (i.e., social media platforms that are able to limit distribution of ads to a 21+ audience only)." — [DISCUS Code PDF](https://distilledspirits.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Final-2025-DISCUS-Code.pdf)
- **2D Responsible Digital Marketing Guidelines: Basic Principles** ("apply to branded digital marketing communications (paid and unpaid)… social network sites and blogs"):
  - "3. Direct interaction by the brand advertiser with a user should require age affirmation by the user prior to direct engagement with that individual."
  - "4. User-generated content on a digital medium controlled by the brand advertiser must be monitored and moderated on a regular basis."
  - "5. Forwardable content should include instructions that such materials should not be forwarded to individuals below the legal purchase age."
  - "7. Digital marketing communications and product promotions must be transparent and overtly identified as brand marketing."
  — [DISCUS Code PDF](https://distilledspirits.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Final-2025-DISCUS-Code.pdf)
- **UGC cadence:** UGC "should be monitored each business day when possible, but, at a minimum, every five business days… A disclaimer should appear saying that all inappropriate content generated by users will be removed". — [DISCUS Code PDF](https://distilledspirits.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Final-2025-DISCUS-Code.pdf)
- **Age affirmation definition:** users "provide their full date of birth (month, day, and year)". — [DISCUS Code PDF](https://distilledspirits.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Final-2025-DISCUS-Code.pdf)
- **Threshold history:** DISCUS raised its standard to 73.8% (from 71.6%) in May 2023, citing 2020 Census data. The 71.6% standard "was grounded in the 2010 Census data". — **[secondary]** [Frankfurt Kurnit (FKKS) Advertising Law blog](https://advertisinglaw.fkks.com/post/102ig73/discus-raises-adult-demographic-placement-standard-to-73-8); [InfoLaw Group, June 2023](https://www.infolawgroup.com/insights/2023/6/5/discus-and-other-spirits-trade-associations-update-adult-demographic-advertising-guidelines)

**Beer Institute Advertising and Marketing Code ("Updated: January 1, 2026"). Verbatim:**
- **3.b** Materials "should not depict situations where beer is being consumed rapidly, excessively, involuntarily, as part of a drinking game, or because of a dare." **3.c** Materials "should not portray persons lacking control over their behavior, movement, or speech because of consuming beer or in any way suggest that such conduct is acceptable." — [Beer Institute Code, 1 Jan 2026 (PDF)](https://www.beerinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/260105_BeerInstitute_Memo_Static_AdGuidelines_Compressed.pdf)
- **5.c (togetherness permitted, necessity barred):** "Beer advertising and marketing materials may portray beer as a part of personal and social interactions and experiences, and a brand may be portrayed in appropriate surroundings as a superior choice to complement a particular occasion or activity. Beer advertising and marketing materials should not, however, claim or represent that individuals cannot obtain social, professional, educational, athletic, or financial success or status without beer consumption." — [Beer Institute Code 2026](https://www.beerinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/260105_BeerInstitute_Memo_Static_AdGuidelines_Compressed.pdf)
- **5.d (problems):** materials "should not claim or represent that individuals cannot solve social, personal, or physical problems without beer consumption." — [Beer Institute Code 2026](https://www.beerinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/260105_BeerInstitute_Memo_Static_AdGuidelines_Compressed.pdf)
- **5.b** "should make no scientifically unsubstantiated health claims." **6.b** "May contain romantic or flirtatious interactions but should not portray sexually explicit activity due to consuming beer." — [Beer Institute Code 2026](https://www.beerinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/260105_BeerInstitute_Memo_Static_AdGuidelines_Compressed.pdf)
- **Humour:** "Humor, parody, satire, and all other advertising themes and devices should be readily identifiable as such by reasonable adult consumers of legal drinking age." — [Beer Institute Code 2026](https://www.beerinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/260105_BeerInstitute_Memo_Static_AdGuidelines_Compressed.pdf)

- **4.c.ii (age-gating, influencers):** "A Brewer will require Social Media Influencers posting beer-branded content on social media platforms to implement age-gating measures, if available on such platforms… A brewer will also require Social Media Influencers to comply with the content-related guidelines of this Code." **4.c.iv:** a "Social Media Influencer" is "a person who posts beer-branded content on a social media platform at a Brewer's request", including celebrities and athletes. — [Beer Institute Code 2026](https://www.beerinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/260105_BeerInstitute_Memo_Static_AdGuidelines_Compressed.pdf)
- **4.d** Semi-annual after-the-fact audits of placements, including "influencer posts". **4.e:** "A Brewer will regularly monitor user-generated content… and beer-branded content from Social Media Influencers for compliance with this Code." **4.g:** "Models and actors… should be at least 25 years old". — [Beer Institute Code 2026](https://www.beerinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/260105_BeerInstitute_Memo_Static_AdGuidelines_Compressed.pdf)
- The Beer Institute released an updated Code on 1 January 2026. — [Beer Institute press release](https://www.beerinstitute.org/press-releases/beer-institute-updates-advertising-code-to-promote-responsible-marketing/)
- Threshold conflict: secondary sources say the Beer Institute adopted a **73.6%** standard in July 2022 — **[secondary]** [FKKS](https://advertisinglaw.fkks.com/post/102ig73/discus-raises-adult-demographic-placement-standard-to-73-8); [InfoLaw Group](https://www.infolawgroup.com/insights/2023/6/5/discus-and-other-spirits-trade-associations-update-adult-demographic-advertising-guidelines). The current January 2026 Code text reads **73.8%** — [Beer Institute Code 2026](https://www.beerinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/260105_BeerInstitute_Memo_Static_AdGuidelines_Compressed.pdf). Treat 73.8% as current and 71.6% and 73.6% as superseded.

### Inferences
- The US codes are a lower bar on mood and "fix" language than the UK. Beer Institute 5.d targets only "cannot solve… without beer" (necessity), not "helps solve". DISCUS bans "curative or therapeutic claims". A "fix" story that implies the drink cures a craving or a bad day would still sit uneasily with DISCUS viii and with Beer Institute 5.d's necessity framing ("need" = "cannot… without").
- The adult-audience thresholds are US-specific (21+). The transferable practice for South Africa (18+) is the logic, not the number. Under the UK standard (CAP 18.15) no more than 25% of the audience may be under 18, which is roughly a 75% adult benchmark. Platform age-gating (section 8) is the practical equivalent.
- The 25+ model rule appears in CAP 18.16, BCAP 19.17, Portman 3.2(i), DISCUS 2B.2 and Beer Institute 4.g. It is a de facto global norm.

### Gaps
- I found no DISCUS line that expressly mentions "stress" or "relieving stress". The closest provisions are viii (therapeutic) and iv (success/status).
- No DISCUS Code Review Board or Beer Institute CCRB decisions on mood or coping claims were retrieved. The decision archives were not searched.

## 5. EU Audiovisual Media Services Directive (2010/13/EU as amended by 2018/1808): Article 9(1)(e) and Article 22

### Takeaway
Article 9(1)(e) is short and general: it covers all audiovisual commercial communications, including on video-sharing platforms via Article 28b. The specific "therapeutic… stimulant, a sedative or a means of resolving personal conflicts" and "social or sexual success" wording is in Article 22, which applies to TV advertising and teleshopping. Many national codes copy Article 22 into all media.

### Cited Findings
- **Article 9(1)(e)**, verbatim: "audiovisual commercial communications for alcoholic beverages shall not be aimed specifically at minors and shall not encourage immoderate consumption of such beverages;" — [AVMSD consolidated text (18.12.2018), EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:02010L0013-20181218)
- **Article 22**, verbatim: "Television advertising and teleshopping for alcoholic beverages shall comply with the following criteria:
  (a) it may not be aimed specifically at minors or, in particular, depict minors consuming these beverages;
  (b) it shall not link the consumption of alcohol to enhanced physical performance or to driving;
  (c) it shall not create the impression that the consumption of alcohol contributes towards social or sexual success;
  (d) it shall not claim that alcohol has therapeutic qualities or that it is a stimulant, a sedative or a means of resolving personal conflicts;
  (e) it shall not encourage immoderate consumption of alcohol or present abstinence or moderation in a negative light;"
  — [AVMSD consolidated text, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:02010L0013-20181218)
- **Article 22(f)** was cut off in the extraction. To my knowledge it reads "it shall not place emphasis on high alcoholic content as being a positive quality of the beverages." **[paraphrase / not verified in this session; verify on EUR-Lex]**
- **Article 28b** (video-sharing platforms), verbatim: "Member States shall ensure that video-sharing platform providers under their jurisdiction comply with the requirements set out in Article 9(1) with respect to audiovisual commercial communications that are marketed, sold or arranged by those video-sharing platform providers." For communications not sold by the platform, providers must "take appropriate measures to comply with the requirements set out in Article 9(1)… taking into account the limited control exercised". — [AVMSD consolidated text, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:02010L0013-20181218)

### Inferences
- Article 22(d), "a means of resolving personal conflicts", is the EU legal ancestor of CAP 18.6 and BCAP 19.7 ("overcome problems"). Article 22(e), "present abstinence or moderation in a negative light", is the ancestor of the UK Dry January rulings (Reserve, VK).
- Through Article 28b, the general Article 9(1)(e) standard reaches TikTok, Instagram Reels and YouTube in the EU. The detailed Article 22 list technically binds TV only.

### Gaps
- The extraction came from the 18 December 2018 consolidated version. Later consolidations (for example after the European Media Freedom Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/1083) were not checked. I am not aware that they altered Articles 9 or 22, but this is unverified.
- The Article 9(3) wording on encouraging co-/self-regulatory codes to limit minors' exposure to alcohol communications was not extracted verbatim.

## 6. IARD Digital Guiding Principles, Influencer Guiding Principles, and WFA Responsible Marketing Pact (global producer commitments)

### Takeaway
The world's largest beer, wine and spirits producers commit to five digital safeguards on every brand channel: an age-affirmation mechanism, a forward advice notice, a responsible drinking message, a transparency message and a UGC policy. For influencers they commit to age-affirmation, or 25+ influencers where platform age-gating is not effective, written agreements, disclosure, and a 72-hour fix-or-remove rule. The WFA's EU Responsible Marketing Pact mirrors these commitments.

### Cited Findings
- **IARD Digital Guiding Principles (DGPs): the five safeguards, verbatim.**
  1. "Age-affirmation mechanism – A mechanism should check that the user is over the country's LPA."
  2. "Forward advice notice – A clear statement that content shouldn't be forwarded to anyone under the LPA in the country of viewing."
  3. "Responsible drinking message – A clearly visible message explicitly promoting responsible consumption."
  4. "Transparency message – It must be clear that the digital marketing communication page is the official brand page."
  5. "User-generated-content policy – A clear statement that any user posts that condone irresponsible drinking will be removed."
  — [IARD, Standards for Responsible Digital Marketing](https://www.iard.org/actions/digital-guiding-principles)
- IARD reports that "Accenture independently assess the DGPs, finding a 98.2% full compliance rate in 2024… This result surpasses the 95% compliance target for 2024". These are self-reported industry figures. — [IARD DGP page](https://www.iard.org/actions/digital-guiding-principles)
- The DGPs respond to a 2018 United Nations call for "concrete steps" towards eliminating marketing of alcohol to minors. Versions dated 2018, 2019, 2023 and 2024 are posted. — [IARD DGP page](https://www.iard.org/actions/digital-guiding-principles); 2024 version: [DGP 2024 PDF](https://www.iard.org/getmedia/d8057d62-6bed-442d-81a3-cc5536ab268e/241111_DGP_2024_A4.pdf)
- **IARD Influencer Guiding Principles (IGPs, 14 September 2021). Verbatim:**
  - Scope: the five safeguards "apply to paid, incentivized, and unpaid influencer content where there is a contractual or other material relationship in place between the producer and the influencer."
  - **1 (Selection):** "Where available, all paid influencers must use age-affirmation mechanisms on digital platforms to prevent minors from seeing this content… When utilizing those platforms where age-affirmation mechanisms for influencers are not yet effective, paid influencers should be aged at least 25 years and primarily appeal to audiences above the legal purchase age."
  - **2:** influencers "should be vetted and, to the best of the producer's knowledge, should have no reputational association with harmful use of alcohol".
  - **3 (Engagement):** a signed written agreement including "(b) Disclosure guidelines… (c) Responsibility guidelines – asking the influencer to comply with the company's responsible marketing code including ensuring that content does not condone or encourage illegal behavior or excessive consumption (d) Best-practice tools… details on how to age restrict their posts".
  - **4 (Monitoring):** "Influencer posts must be monitored by brands or their agencies for compliance and the influencer should fix or remove them within 72 hours if they are not compliant."
  - **5:** "Brands should regularly audit and monitor campaigns for compliance."
  — [IARD Influencer Guiding Principles PDF](https://www.iard.org/getmedia/fa30bdb7-0567-4da8-87b1-50abc47fc1ac/14092021-Influencer-Guiding-Principles-FINAL_1.pdf)
- The IGPs define influencer content as marketing (not UGC) "when the influencer has received compensation through financial remuneration or there has been some form of editorial control by the advertisers (European Advertising Standards Alliance (EASA) definition)". — [IARD IGPs PDF](https://www.iard.org/getmedia/fa30bdb7-0567-4da8-87b1-50abc47fc1ac/14092021-Influencer-Guiding-Principles-FINAL_1.pdf)
- 13 agencies (including dentsu, Havas, McCann London and Publicis Groupe) signed the IARD Influencer Pledge. — [IARD Influencer marketing page](https://www.iard.org/actions/Influencer-Guiding-Principles)
- **WFA Responsible Marketing Pact (RMP, EU), influencer commitments. Verbatim:**
  - "Selection: influencers should be over 25 years of age unless robust age-affirmation mechanisms are in place, and they should not have any reputational association with harmful alcohol use."
  - "Review: signatories should consider monitoring influencer posts and correct or remove posts… within 72hrs of becoming aware."
  - "Content: influencers working with alcohol brands should not portray excessive or irresponsible consumption and any practices and techniques that are likely to be disproportionately appealing to minors."
  - The commitment "also covers advertising for alcohol alternatives."
  — [WFA RMP, Influencer marketing](https://rmp.wfanet.org/influencer-marketing)
- A large producer's own policy applies the forward advice rule as: "Contain a forward advice notice that reminds those over LPA of their responsibility to not share content containing alcohol with those under the LPA." — [Coca-Cola Company, Responsible Alcohol Marketing Policy](https://www.coca-colacompany.com/policies-and-practices/legal-and-practices/responsible-alcohol-marketing-policy)

### Inferences
- The DGP five-point checklist works as a profile and story hygiene list for Doña Fuego's Instagram:
  - Age gate (Meta's alcohol age restriction set to 18+).
  - Forward-advice line in the bio or highlights ("Don't share with anyone under 18").
  - A responsible drinking message on posts and stories.
  - A verified, clearly official brand page.
  - A pinned UGC/community policy.
- The IGPs cover the content side only indirectly, by requiring influencers to follow the company's own code. So the do's and don'ts one-pager is itself the instrument the IGPs expect to be attached to every influencer agreement.

### Gaps
- I did not locate an IARD document titled "Responsible Marketing Pledge". IARD's "Producers' Commitments" (2013) and the "Influencer Pledge" exist ([IARD Influencer page](https://www.iard.org/actions/Influencer-Guiding-Principles)), and the WFA "Responsible Marketing Pact" covers the EU. One of these may be what was meant; the exact title was not confirmed.
- The 2024 DGP PDF itself was not opened. The safeguards quoted come from IARD's DGP web page.
- IARD's global *content* principles (for example the "Guiding Principles: Self-Regulation of Marketing Communications" document) were not fetched, so any IARD wording on mood or social success is not quoted here.

## 7. WHO SAFER and the Global Alcohol Action Plan 2022–2030: where regulation is heading (brief context)

### Takeaway
The WHO's position is that marketing restrictions, explicitly including "the digital world", are a cost-effective "best buy". Its European office has flagged "dark" (individually targeted) social ads and weak age verification as the gaps. The direction of travel is toward stricter platform age assurance, ad labelling and legal (not just self-regulatory) limits on digital alcohol marketing.

### Cited Findings
- **WHO SAFER**, verbatim: "Enforce bans or comprehensive restrictions on alcohol advertising, sponsorship, and promotion. Bans and comprehensive restrictions on alcohol advertising, sponsorship and promotion are impactful and cost-effective measures. Enacting and enforcing bans or comprehensive restrictions on exposure to them in the digital world will bring public health benefits and help protect children, adolescents and abstainers from the pressure to start consuming alcohol." — [WHO SAFER initiative](https://www.who.int/initiatives/SAFER)
- SAFER was "launched in 2018 by WHO and partners". — [WHO alcohol fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/alcohol)
- The **Global alcohol action plan 2022–2030** was published 20 June 2024 and endorsed by Member States. — [WHO, Global alcohol action plan 2022–2030](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240090101); [WHO action plan page](https://www.who.int/teams/mental-health-and-substance-use/alcohol-drugs-and-addictive-behaviours/alcohol/our-activities/towards-and-action-plan-on-alcohol)
- An academic analysis says "The GAAP now requires alcohol companies to act 'stringently' within their core roles, for example in restricting marketing exposure to minors… and to desist from existing forms of corporate social responsibility activities". It also notes that digital marketing "is inherently cross-national in character". — **[secondary]** [BMJ Global Health analysis of the GAAP](https://gh.bmj.com/content/9/2/e014246)
- **WHO/Europe digital marketing report (8 December 2021):** "Only 14 Member States of the WHO European Region indicated that they have a ban on alcohol marketing on the Internet and social media". Dr Carina Ferreira-Borges: "Age verification schemes, where they exist, are usually inadequate to protect minors… the vast majority of alcohol advertising online is 'dark', in the sense that it is only visible to the consumer to whom it is marketed". Policy options listed: "Effective age verification systems for use of platforms; Clear labelling of advertisements in social media posts; Use of algorithm-based processes for alcohol-related brand names, so as to block access; Sanctions for inappropriate activities, with robust enforcement". — [WHO/Europe news release](https://www.who.int/europe/news/item/08-12-2021-who-report-on-digital-marketing-of-alcohol-control-policies-in-the-european-region-are-not-enough)
- WHO technical report on cross-border (largely digital) alcohol marketing (2022). — [WHO technical report page](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240046504)

### Inferences
- Self-regulatory codes (Portman, DISCUS, IARD) are increasingly framed by the WHO as insufficient. Brands that adopt the strictest available benchmark (UK CAP/Portman content rules plus IARD digital safeguards) are positioned ahead of probable tightening, in South Africa included.

### Gaps
- The verbatim GAAP text on marketing (action area and targets) was not extracted. Only the secondary BMJ summary is cited.

## 8. Platform policies: Meta (Instagram/Facebook), TikTok, Google/YouTube

### Takeaway
- **Meta's** alcohol ad and branded-content policies are *targeting* rules (18+ minimum; South Africa not listed, so 18+ applies). On content they defer to "applicable local laws, required or established industry codes". They do not themselves mention excessive drinking or alcohol as a solution.
- **TikTok no longer bans alcohol ads outright.** It allows them market by market via a sales rep. South Africa is allowed with 18+ restriction, registration documents and disclaimers. Alcohol branded content is allowed and age-restricted to 18+ in South Africa. Globally, TikTok bars under-25s, excessive drinking and "offering alcohol as a prize or reward".
- **Google/YouTube** has the most UK-like content rules: alcohol must not "produce social or sexual success or acceptance", be "a means of removing social or sexual inhibitions", have "therapeutic properties", or be "necessary for a successful or healthy lifestyle".

### Cited Findings

**Meta: Alcohol ad policy** (Transparency Center; change log shows 26 June 2024):
- Verbatim: "Ads that promote or reference alcohol must comply with all applicable local laws, required or established industry codes, guidelines, licenses and approvals, and include age and country targeting criteria consistent with Meta's targeting requirements and applicable local laws." And: "At a minimum, ads may not be targeted to people under 18 years of age." — [Meta Ad Standards: Alcohol](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/alcohol)
- Ads that "promote alcohol" include "Depiction of alcohol brands/logos", "Consumption of alcohol beverages", "Promotion of alcohol brands", "Recipes for alcoholic beverages" and "Availability of alcohol sales". — [Meta Ad Standards: Alcohol](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/alcohol)
- Countries with higher ages include the United States (21) and Sweden (25). Otherwise: "Advertisers that promote alcohol products or brands in countries not included in the permitted list above, may promote alcohol products, as long as they comply with their local laws and target people who are at least 18 years old." South Africa is on neither the prohibited nor the age-specific list. — [Meta Ad Standards: Alcohol](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/alcohol)
- The policy text contains no clause on excessive drinking, mood, coping or alcohol as a solution. Content standards are imported through "required or established industry codes". — [Meta Ad Standards: Alcohol](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/alcohol)

**Meta: Branded Content Policies, Alcohol.** Verbatim: "Branded content promoting or referencing alcohol must comply with all applicable local laws, required or established industry codes, guidelines, licenses and approvals, and include age and country targeting criteria consistent with Facebook's targeting guidelines and applicable local laws. Branded content must be age-gated to 18 years or older in all permitted countries except as follows: Prohibited: Afghanistan, Brunei, Bangladesh, Egypt, Estonia, Gambia, India, Kuwait, Libya, Lithuania, Norway, Pakistan, Russia, Saudi Arabia, Turkey, United Arab Emirates and Yemen. 25 years or older: Sweden. 21 years or older: Cameroon, Micronesia, Palau, Solomon Islands, Sri Lanka and the United States…" — [Meta Business Help Center, Branded Content Policies](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/221149188908254); Instagram equivalent: [Instagram Help, Branded Content Policies](https://help.instagram.com/1695974997209192)

**TikTok: Alcohol ad policy** ("Last updated: September 2025"). Verbatim:
- Advertisers must "Clearly state the alcoholic content and strength of a beverage and carry a responsible drinking disclaimer on ad content and landing pages." — [TikTok Ads Policy: Alcohol](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/tiktok-ads-policy-alcohol)
- "Examples of what is not allowed in ad content or on landing pages: Targeting or appealing to people below the legal drinking age; Featuring people below the age of 25; Featuring pregnant people; Portrayal of excessive drinking, intoxication, or reckless behavior under the influence of alcohol; Offering alcohol as a prize or reward, or promoting any offers or incentives encouraging the consumption of alcohol." — [TikTok Ads Policy: Alcohol](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/tiktok-ads-policy-alcohol)
- **South Africa (market-specific), verbatim:** "Alcohol-related events and establishments: Not allowed. Examples of what is not allowed: Bars." And: "Alcohol and related products: May be allowed if the following requirements are met: Work with a TikTok Sales Representative; Provide necessary registration documents; Include appropriate disclaimers; The ad must be restricted to users aged 18 years and older. Examples of what may be allowed: Alcoholic beverages, such as beer, liquor or spirits, wine. Examples of what is not allowed: Alcohol delivery services". — [TikTok: Alcohol market-specific requirements](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/alcohol-market-specific-requirements)

The current per-market rules are on the official page above.

Verbatim: "Alcohol - Including alcoholic beverages (wine, beer, spirits, etc.), alcohol clubs and subscription services, alcohol-making kits and alcohol-sponsored events. This also includes alcohol-free or no-alcohol alternatives, soft drinks presented as mixers for alcohol, drinking accessories or games and content sponsored by alcohol brands. In the following markets, this Branded Content will be age restricted to the appropriate audiences and to the market in which it was originally posted… Audiences aged 18 years and older in Argentina, Australia, Brazil, Cambodia, Colombia, Germany, Israel, Italy, Kenya, Mexico, Philippines, Nigeria, Singapore, Spain, **South Africa**, Taiwan, United Kingdom, Vietnam". YouTube is a permitted platform, with age gating available for channels and videos. — [Google Ads policy: Alcohol](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6012382?hl=en)

### Inferences
- On Instagram, Meta will not catch a "fix" story on content grounds. Its policy only polices age and country targeting and outsources content standards to "established industry codes". The content benchmark therefore has to come from the codes in sections 1–6 plus South African law and ARA rules (other researcher).
- Google's "necessary for a successful or healthy lifestyle" is the platform-policy twin of CAP's "need (as opposed to merely enjoy)" and Portman's "needs, as opposed to likes". Three independent sources draw the same line against need framing.
- TikTok South Africa bars "alcohol delivery services" ads. Inference: a TikTok ad whose call to action is "order on Sixty60" could be read as promoting delivery and should be checked with the TikTok rep. This is unverified in practice.
- TikTok's "Offering alcohol as a prize or reward" is about competitions and incentives, not messaging. Combined with the UK rulings, though, it supports keeping "treat yourself / reward yourself / you earned it" framing away from the drink.
