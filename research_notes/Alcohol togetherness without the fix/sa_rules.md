# South African rules on alcohol brand content on social media (as at October 2026)

Scope: what a South African alcohol brand's Instagram/Facebook feed, stories, reels, captions, reposts and competitions may and may not say or show. The notes are written for Doña Fuego: a 7% ABV tequila RTD in 250ml cans, with a non-alcoholic Margarita under the same brand, owned by Doña Distillery (Pty) Ltd, Cape Town. The trigger is the story line "get your Fuego fix".

How the sources were handled: clause text was taken from primary PDFs wherever they could be reached. The DF-SA 2026 PDF was machine-extracted, and the extraction ran a few neighbouring clauses together (2.1.7/2.1.8, 2.1.9/2.1.10, 2.2.1/2.2.2, 2.4.1/2.4.2, 2.7.1/2.7.2, 2.9.2/2.9.3, 2.11.1/2.11.2, 2.5.9, 3.3.6.2/3.3.6.3). Those clauses were rebuilt against the near-identical wording in the ARB's Alcohol Appendix and are marked "(rebuilt)". The wording of every other quoted clause is unchanged from the extract.

## 1. Which industry code is current (the ARA code's successor), whom it binds, and how enforcement and pre-clearance work

### Takeaway
The ARA Code of Commercial Communication no longer exists. The ARA was succeeded by Aware.org, whose code (launched 28 January 2020) was in turn adopted by the Drinks Federation of South Africa (DF-SA). The current text is the DF-SA "Alcohol Industry Communications Code of Conduct", posted as "Code-of-Conduct-2026.pdf" in July 2026. The ARB reproduces it as its Alcohol Appendix (file v2026-02, headed "(2025)"). The code formally binds only DF-SA and Aware.org members. Doña Distillery is not on the DF-SA member list. The ARB can still rule on a non-member's ad and refer it to the Minister or Liquor Board, and it can tell its media members not to carry the ad. It cannot bind a non-member that does not consent.

### Cited Findings
- **Lineage.** Aware.org launched its new Code of Commercial Communications on 28 January 2020: "It replaces the previous Code of Commercial Communication of Aware.org's predecessor, namely the Industry Association for Responsible Alcohol Use (ARA)." — [Adams & Adams, 31 Dec 2019](https://www.adams.africa/jenny-pienaar/developments-in-alcohol-advertising-how-we-market-today-will-determine-if-we-can-market-tomorrow/)
- **DF-SA code preamble:** "The August 2023 edition of the Code of Conduct has been adapted from the Aware.org code that it had previously enforced. Oversight of Commercial Communication now falls to DF-SA, while Aware.org will continue with public service communications focused on responsible drinking and social awareness educational initiatives. The Code will be reviewed every three years (or earlier if the need arises) … Companies shall implement its provisions incrementally into their operations but no later than one year from the adoption of this Code of Conduct." — [DF-SA Code of Conduct (PDF uploaded 2026/07)](https://www.drinksfederationsa.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Code-of-Conduct-2026.pdf)
- **Letter and spirit.** The preamble says: "Compliance entails adherence to both the letter and spirit of the Code of Conduct." — [DF-SA Code 2026](https://www.drinksfederationsa.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Code-of-Conduct-2026.pdf)
- **Version confusion.** The 2026 PDF's preamble still calls itself the "August 2023 edition". Its table of contents, however, includes a new section 2.5, "Influencers on Social Media and Emerging Platforms (Paid Content Only)". That section is absent from the ARB's Alcohol Appendix, which is headed "ALCOHOL INDUSTRY CODE OF CONDUCT IN RESPECT TO MARKETING AND COMMUNICATIONS (2025)" and filed as v2026-02. — [DF-SA Code 2026](https://www.drinksfederationsa.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Code-of-Conduct-2026.pdf); [ARB Appendix Alcohol v2026-02](https://arb.org.za/code-file-downloads/Appendix_Alcohol_v2026-02.pdf)
- **Other published versions (not read):** a DF-SA 2024 PDF ([cdn link](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/6540a8e1ee4ed9c757909480/6628b98771b47c53eb8f4a46_Communications%20Code%20of%20Conduct%20-%20FINAL%20-%202024.pdf)) and an earlier ARB copy ([ARB "2023 – ARB version final Mar 2024"](https://www.arb.org.za/assets/alcohol-industry-code-of-conduct_-marketing-and-communications-2023---arb-version-final-mar-2024.pdf)).
- **Scope, cl. 1.1:** "This Code of Conduct applies to all DF-SA and Aware.org members. By their commitment to the Code, they undertake that their commercial communication activities are and will be, at all times, responsible, legal, decent, honest, and truthful, conforming to good, ethical business practices." — [DF-SA Code 2026](https://www.drinksfederationsa.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Code-of-Conduct-2026.pdf)
- **Scope, cl. 1.2:** "This Code of Conduct governs all forms of brand marketing, promotion, and advertising of alcoholic beverages in all media including, but not limited to, television, radio, print, billboards, digital and social media, direct marketing, text messaging, packaging, brand promotions, brand public relations activities (paid or unpaid), experiential events, brand event sponsorships, product placement, merchandising, and point-of-sale ("Commercial Communication")." — [DF-SA Code 2026](https://www.drinksfederationsa.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Code-of-Conduct-2026.pdf)
- **Exclusions, cl. 1.4:** "This Code of Conduct does not apply to public service communications dedicated solely to curtailing alcohol abuse; corporate communications conveying factual company information; user-generated content on social media platforms or pages that are unaffiliated with a producer; or to Commercial Communications for alcohol-free or non-alcohol beverages, provided those beverages do not share a brand name with an alcohol product and comply with Section 2.10." — [DF-SA Code 2026](https://www.drinksfederationsa.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Code-of-Conduct-2026.pdf)
- **DF-SA members** listed on the DF-SA site (page modified 26 Feb 2026): DGB, Diageo SA, Halewood SA, HEINEKEN South Africa, Pernod Ricard SA, The Really Great Brand Company (RGBC) and South African Breweries. — [DF-SA home](https://www.drinksfederationsa.co.za/home/)
- **Enforcement, cl. 3.1.1:** "The ARB shall be the arbitrator that decides on the validity of alleged Section 2, Commercial Communications, violations raised by both members of the public and competitors." — [DF-SA Code 2026](https://www.drinksfederationsa.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Code-of-Conduct-2026.pdf)
- **Non-members, cl. 3.2.1:** "Where a business is not a member of its sector's trade association and/or has not signed onto this Code of Conduct, the ARB may proceed with adjudicating alleged Code of Conduct violations against that business and, if found to be in violation, shall give notice to the relevant Minister for review and resolution." — [DF-SA Code 2026](https://www.drinksfederationsa.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Code-of-Conduct-2026.pdf)
- **Non-members, cl. 3.3.6.3 (rebuilt):** "Where the offending business is not a signatory to this Code of Conduct in its own right or through its trade association, the ARB shall nonetheless render a decision along with a recommendation of the appropriate sanction and then refer the matter to the appropriate Minister, Liquor Board, and/or the Department for independent review and enforcement." — [DF-SA Code 2026](https://www.drinksfederationsa.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Code-of-Conduct-2026.pdf). The ARB Appendix's cl. 2.2.3.2 carries the same text without the referral to the Minister. — [ARB Appendix Alcohol](https://arb.org.za/code-file-downloads/Appendix_Alcohol_v2026-02.pdf)
- **Sanctions, cl. 3.3.6.1–3.3.6.2 (rebuilt):** sanctions take into account "whether the business sought pre-clearance advice that it subsequently followed, whether the business is a repeat offender…". They range from "a written warning for a first offence, amendment or withdrawal of the offending advertising and marketing, corrective advertising, and, in the most egregious cases of a repeat offender, the ban of all marketing and advertising activity for a period of up to three months." — [DF-SA Code 2026](https://www.drinksfederationsa.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Code-of-Conduct-2026.pdf)
- **Pre-clearance, cl. 3.3.4.1:** "The ARB desires, in the first instance, to prevent breaches of the Code of Conduct's Commercial Communication provisions. For that reason, an optional, non-binding pre-clearance advisory service is to be made available to signatories and to those businesses whose license is conditioned upon complying with this Code of Conduct." — [DF-SA Code 2026](https://www.drinksfederationsa.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Code-of-Conduct-2026.pdf)
- **No prior approval under the Aware.org code.** That code's Marketing Sub-Committee "will not have the power of prior approval of alcohol marketing material, but are mandated to review marketing activities, act on complaints and arbitrate in disputes". — [Aware.org Code of Commercial Communications toolkit](https://assets-global.website-files.com/64afbf0da65d7b3be0358d54/64c9132703ce31f755a5ee3a_Code-Toolkit-1.pdf)
- **Complaint routes, ARB Appendix cl. 2.2.1.1 and 2.2.1.3:** "Consumers may approach either the DF-SA or the ARB with complaints about the contents of Commercial Communications." Where a complaint is not resolved, "the complainant may at any point of the process refer the matter to the ARB for review and decision." — [ARB Appendix Alcohol](https://arb.org.za/code-file-downloads/Appendix_Alcohol_v2026-02.pdf)
- **Industry complaints in practice.** The DF-SA itself lodged the 2024 complaint against the Don Billiato tequila ad. ARB CEO Gail Schimmel said: "This is a case of the industry itself saying we are not prepared to put up with this type of advertising." — [News24, 18 Jul 2024](https://www.news24.com/business/companies/very-much-a-party-vibe-arb-orders-cassper-nyovests-tequila-brand-to-pull-ad-glamourising-drinking-20240718)

### Inferences
- Doña Distillery is not on the DF-SA member list. The code therefore does not bind it contractually. In practice it is still exposed: the code is the ARB's Alcohol Appendix; DF-SA and consumers do file complaints (Don Billiato was a non-member brand); the ARB will rule and refer non-members to the Minister or Liquor Board; and Meta requires alcohol ads and branded content to comply with "required or established industry codes" (see section 9). Treating the code as binding is the safe operating assumption.
- The "spirit" clause in the preamble undercuts arguments that a line is technically outside the wording, such as "'fix' isn't listed".

### Gaps
- No DF-SA web page was found that states the formal adoption date of the 2026 text or summarises what changed. The PDF's own preamble still says "August 2023 edition".
- The ARB's pre-clearance service is described as available to "signatories" and to licence-conditioned businesses. I could not confirm whether a non-member such as Doña Distillery can use it, what it costs or how long it takes. I also could not confirm whether Doña's liquor registration is conditioned on code compliance.
- No current ARA or Aware.org pre-vetting service was found. The old ARA pre-vetting arrangements could not be located.

## 2. Clause wording on alcohol as a fix, need, cure, therapy, stress relief, problem-solver, reward or route to social success (priority)

### Takeaway
No clause in any South African source names the words "fix", "need", "self-care", "earned it" or "therapy". Three DF-SA clauses squarely cover the idea:
- cl. 2.4.2: drinking cannot be shown to cause success, "solve personal problems" or "enable social acceptance";
- cl. 2.7.4: alcohol cannot be offered as having "curative qualities" or as a "stimulant, sedative, or tranquiliser";
- cl. 2.8.2: content cannot "glamourise illicit drugs, drug culture".

The ARB enforced cl. 2.4.2 (cl. 1.4.1 in its Appendix) against Strongbow in 2025 for showing cider relieving work stress. The code expressly permits drinking shown "as a relaxed, sociable, and enjoyable part of a well-lived life" and everyday social occasions.

### Cited Findings
- **Success, problems, inhibitions, acceptance, DF-SA cl. 2.4.2 (rebuilt):** "Commercial Communications should not imply that drinking resulted in business, social, professional, academic, athletic, financial, or other personal success, nor that drinking can solve personal problems, overcome inhibitions, shyness, or social barriers, or enable social acceptance." — [DF-SA Code 2026](https://www.drinksfederationsa.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Code-of-Conduct-2026.pdf). ARB Appendix cl. 1.4.1 is identical apart from "shyness or social barriers". — [ARB Appendix Alcohol](https://arb.org.za/code-file-downloads/Appendix_Alcohol_v2026-02.pdf)
- **What is permitted, DF-SA cl. 2.4.1:** "Commercial Communications may reflect South Africa's diversity of people from all walks of life enjoying various everyday social experiences and occasions." — [DF-SA Code 2026](https://www.drinksfederationsa.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Code-of-Conduct-2026.pdf)
- **Positive framing, DF-SA cl. 2.2.1:** "Commercial Communications that depict responsible drinking among adults as a relaxed, sociable, and enjoyable part of a well-lived life have a role to play in reinforcing a responsible approach to drinking." — [DF-SA Code 2026](https://www.drinksfederationsa.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Code-of-Conduct-2026.pdf)
- **Therapeutic, stimulant and sedative, DF-SA cl. 2.7.4:** "Commercial Communications may not claim that alcohol has curative qualities or offer alcohol as a performance enhancer, stimulant, sedative, or tranquiliser." — [DF-SA Code 2026](https://www.drinksfederationsa.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Code-of-Conduct-2026.pdf). Same text in ARB Appendix cl. 1.6.4. — [ARB Appendix Alcohol](https://arb.org.za/code-file-downloads/Appendix_Alcohol_v2026-02.pdf)
- **Health claims, DF-SA cl. 2.7.3:** "Commercial Communications for alcohol brands may not make or imply health claims or suggest potential net health benefits from consuming certain forms of alcohol, nor may they include terms like "healthier," "better for you," or "safer."" — [DF-SA Code 2026](https://www.drinksfederationsa.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Code-of-Conduct-2026.pdf)
- **Paid influencer content, DF-SA cl. 2.5.4.** It "must not … 2.5.4.3. Refer to any health benefits associated with consuming alcoholic beverages. 2.5.4.4. Suggest that alcohol consumption improves mental, physical, or sporting performance. 2.5.4.5. Imply that drinking alcohol contributes to social, sexual, or personal success. 2.5.4.6. Glamorise or trivialise irresponsible alcohol consumption." — [DF-SA Code 2026](https://www.drinksfederationsa.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Code-of-Conduct-2026.pdf)
- **Drug culture, DF-SA cl. 2.8.2:** "Commercial communication may not portray or in any way endorse or glamourise illicit drugs, drug culture, organised crime, or local gang activity." — [DF-SA Code 2026](https://www.drinksfederationsa.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Code-of-Conduct-2026.pdf)
- **Brand names and packaging, DF-SA cl. 2.10.1:** "Brand names or product packaging that conveys sexual innuendo, the intoxicating effect of alcohol, or that use colloquialisms for alcohol (e.g., "dop," "booze," or "grog"), illegal activity, alcohol abuse, violence, illicit drugs or drug culture, gang terminology, or any other activities regarded by a reasonable person to be harmful to individuals or communities is prohibited." — [DF-SA Code 2026](https://www.drinksfederationsa.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Code-of-Conduct-2026.pdf)
- **Celebrity success, DF-SA cl. 7.10.2:** "The perception may not be created that alcohol beverages or the consumption of alcohol resulted in a celebrity's fame or success or played any role in the achievements of a sports or popular entertainment personality." — [DF-SA Code 2026](https://www.drinksfederationsa.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Code-of-Conduct-2026.pdf)
- **Performance under the earlier Aware.org code:** "Commercial communication may not suggest that alcohol is required for physical proficiency or capability, or that increased ability or strength results from consuming alcohol beverages." — [Aware.org Code toolkit](https://assets-global.website-files.com/64afbf0da65d7b3be0358d54/64c9132703ce31f755a5ee3a_Code-Toolkit-1.pdf)
- **Ruling: Strongbow Gold Apple cider, TV ad, reported 5 June 2025. Upheld; withdrawal ordered.**
  - The ad showed a stressed office worker buried in paperwork. A paper aeroplane turned into a Strongbow bottle, the worker relaxed with his feet on the desk, and screens showed 17:00 with the line "Never stop playing".
  - The advertiser argued the scene was a metaphor for "leaving work behind". The ARB found viewers "would reasonably" read it "as portraying alcohol consumption as a solution to workplace stress".
  - The ARB singled out "the stressed worker visibly transitioning to a state of happiness after the introduction of the cider". This "directly ties emotional relief and relaxation to the product", contrary to cl. 1.4.1.
  - The advertiser was "ordered to withdraw the advertisement and prohibited from using it again in its current form."
  - Caveat: News24 paraphrases cl. 1.4.1 as prohibiting "any suggestion … that alcohol consumption can reduce stress, improve relaxation…". The clause text does not mention stress or relaxation. That is the reporter's gloss, not the clause wording.
  — [News24, 5 Jun 2025](https://www.news24.com/business/consumer-lookout/strongbow-ad-promotes-booze-to-help-manage-work-stress-watchdog-finds-20250605-0715)
- **Ruling: Don Billiato tequila (Blue Sky Brand Company; Cassper Nyovest), TV on DStv channel 203 on 11 and 14 April 2024, plus Instagram. Complaint by DF-SA. Partially upheld; ad pulled.**
  - The tagline was "enjoy a taste of wealth". The DF-SA said it implied "the alcohol brand had resulted in personal success".
  - The ARB upheld the complaint on celebrity use/primary appeal and on "the association of personal success with drinking". It dismissed the complaint about attire.
  - Schimmel: "this association of wealth and glamour with drinking and social success with drinking that's pervasive throughout the advertisement is a problem."
  - The advertiser's "tongue in cheek" defence failed.
  — [News24, 18 Jul 2024](https://www.news24.com/business/companies/very-much-a-party-vibe-arb-orders-cassper-nyovests-tequila-brand-to-pull-ad-glamourising-drinking-20240718)
- **Ruling: Wildebeest Brandewyn (Bundu Brands), TV, ruled 11 May 2026. Dismissed.**
  - The line was "Brandy is like love – the more you get, the happier you are."
  - The Directorate acknowledged "significant discomfort" but found the tone comedic and nostalgic, and that viewers were unlikely to take it literally.
  - The ruling said alcohol advertising "is legally permitted to use positive associations to market products, provided it does not explicitly promise success or claim to solve personal problems". It cautioned that the ad "could have been more thoughtfully scripted".
  — [Bizcommunity, 21 May 2026](https://www.bizcommunity.com/article/arb-clears-wildebeest-brandewyn-ad-accused-of-promoting-binge-drinking-067655a)
- **Dictionary sense of "fix".** Cambridge gives the noun "fix (DRUG)", slang: "an amount of an illegal drug, or of another substance that has an effect on someone", with the example "He was shaking badly and needed a fix." A second sense is "an amount of a something that someone needs". — [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/fix)

### Inferences
- **"Get your Fuego fix".** The ordinary meaning of "fix" is a dose of a drug or substance someone needs. Read that way, the line frames the product as something craved or depended on. It sits close to the drug-culture bar in cl. 2.8.2 and the sedative/tranquiliser/curative bar in cl. 2.7.4. If paired with a bad-day or stress set-up, it also falls under cl. 2.4.2's "solve personal problems", and Strongbow shows the ARB reads implied emotional relief strictly.
- **How a complaint would likely go.** Wildebeest shows humour and context can save a borderline line, but the ARB still voiced "significant discomfort". On balance the line is a moderate-risk breach of the code's spirit rather than a clear breach of its letter. This is my reading, not a ruling.
- **Wording patterns to avoid,** each with the clause behind it:
  - "need", "fix", "cure", "therapy", "medicine", "self-care", "survive [Monday]", "unwind after a stressful day", "earned it" or "reward yourself" after hardship: cl. 2.4.2 and 2.7.4.
  - Before/after mood transformations, the Strongbow pattern: cl. 2.4.2.
  - "Confidence in a can" or "the life of the party": overcoming inhibitions or shyness, and social acceptance, cl. 2.4.2.
  - Wealth or status taglines, the Don Billiato pattern: cl. 2.4.2.
  - Energy, "pick-me-up" or performance claims: cl. 2.7.4 and 2.5.4.4.
- **What remains permitted:** depicting adults enjoying the product at everyday social occasions, such as togetherness, food or a braai. That is cl. 2.2.1 and 2.4.1, provided the drink is not shown as the cause of the good time, success or acceptance.

### Gaps
- No ARB ruling text was obtained directly. The ARB rulings database is an Airtable embed that could not be queried, so the Strongbow and Don Billiato details come from press reports. The Strongbow ruling date and advertiser name were not reported in the source read.
- No South African ruling or guidance was found on "fix", "need", "self-care", "survive", "earned it" or "therapy" specifically.

## 3. Excessive or rapid consumption, pressure to drink, abstinence; driving, sport, dangerous activities, pregnancy

### Takeaway
The code bars depicting intoxication, rapid or excessive drinking (including "boat races" and "down-downs"), drinking before or during skilled activities (driving, machinery, sport), visibly pregnant women and athletes drinking. It requires that drinking be presented as a personal choice, never disparaging abstainers. The Brutal Fruit beach ad was withdrawn in 2025, and the ARB's "significant discomfort" over Wildebeest's "the more you get, the happier you are" shows how quantity language is read.

### Cited Findings
- **Loss of control, DF-SA cl. 2.2.2:** "Commercial Communications should never show drinkers as losing control or having diminished movement, speech, or vision due to drinking alcohol." — [DF-SA Code 2026](https://www.drinksfederationsa.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Code-of-Conduct-2026.pdf)
- **Skilled activities, DF-SA cl. 2.2.3:** "Commercial Communications must not show or imply drinking before or during activities requiring a high degree of skill, concentration, or sobriety, including driving, operating machinery, sporting, or other athletic activities." — [DF-SA Code 2026](https://www.drinksfederationsa.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Code-of-Conduct-2026.pdf)
- **Pregnancy, DF-SA cl. 2.2.4:** "Commercial Communications may not depict or include visibly pregnant women." — [DF-SA Code 2026](https://www.drinksfederationsa.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Code-of-Conduct-2026.pdf)
- **Abstinence, DF-SA cl. 2.2.5:** "Commercial Communications should make it clear that drinking is a personal choice and never disparage people who choose to abstain from drinking or consume alcohol responsibly." — [DF-SA Code 2026](https://www.drinksfederationsa.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Code-of-Conduct-2026.pdf)
- **Abstinence under the earlier Aware.org code:** "Commercial communication may not present abstinence or moderate consumption of alcohol in a negative light." and "may not portray the encouragement of irresponsible alcohol consumption or feature risky and/or excessive consumption." — [Aware.org Code toolkit](https://assets-global.website-files.com/64afbf0da65d7b3be0358d54/64c9132703ce31f755a5ee3a_Code-Toolkit-1.pdf)
- **Rapid consumption, DF-SA cl. 2.3.1:** "…nor may any brand promotions, events or competitions encourage the rapid, irresponsible, or excessive consumption of alcohol (e.g., "boat races" or "down-downs)." — [DF-SA Code 2026](https://www.drinksfederationsa.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Code-of-Conduct-2026.pdf)
- **Personal responsibility, DF-SA cl. 2.3.4:** "Consumers who attend brand promotions must be encouraged to assume personal responsibility for their decision to drink or not to drink." — [DF-SA Code 2026](https://www.drinksfederationsa.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Code-of-Conduct-2026.pdf)
- **Athletes, DF-SA cl. 2.4.4 and 2.4.5:** "No Commercial Communications may show an athlete or former athlete consuming alcohol." Winning-result celebrations "after the competition has finished" are allowed if athletes are, and appear to be, of legal drinking age. — [DF-SA Code 2026](https://www.drinksfederationsa.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Code-of-Conduct-2026.pdf)
- **Violence, DF-SA cl. 2.8.1:** "Commercial communication may not portray or imply that aggressive, violent or anti-social behaviour is ever appropriate, even as part of a joke or prank." — [DF-SA Code 2026](https://www.drinksfederationsa.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Code-of-Conduct-2026.pdf)
- **Paid influencers, DF-SA cl. 2.5.4.2 and 2.5.4.7:** they must not "Depict or encourage excessive, irresponsible, or illegal alcohol consumption" or "Depict alcohol consumption in connection with unsafe, high-risk, or illegal activities, including but not limited to dangerous stunts, unsafe transport practices, gambling, or similar risky behaviour." — [DF-SA Code 2026](https://www.drinksfederationsa.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Code-of-Conduct-2026.pdf)
- **Ruling: Brutal Fruit (SAB), TV on DStv channel 121, reported 6 Feb 2025.** The ad showed a bottle on a beach towel and friends enjoying the drink. The ARB upheld a consumer complaint about promoting drinking on the beach and "asked that The South African Breweries (SAB) withdraw or amend the advert." The clauses applied were not reported. — [IOL, 6 Feb 2025](https://iol.co.za/news/south-africa/2025-02-06-brutal-fruit-advert-condemned-for-promoting-illegal-beach-drinking/)
- **Western Cape vehicle and service-station offences.**
  - WC Liquor Act s 80(1) makes it an offence for a person who "(a) consumes liquor in a motor vehicle driven or parked on a public road; (b) allows the consumption of liquor in a motor vehicle of which he or she is the driver or which is under his or her control while it is being driven on a public road; or (c) allows liquor contained in an open container to be in a motor vehicle while it is being driven on a public road".
  - s 81(1) ("Offence regarding service stations"): "No liquor may be sold, supplied or consumed on an erf upon which the business of a service station selling petrol, diesel and other petroleum products to the public is conducted."

  — [Western Cape Liquor Act 4 of 2008 (as amended to 2010)](https://www.sawis.co.za/winelaw/download/Western_Cape_Liquor_Act,_as_amended.pdf)

### Inferences
- For Doña Fuego content:
  - avoid multi-can stacks framed as "one person's" haul, and "smash", "down", "send it" or chugging cues;
  - avoid beach or public-place drinking scenes (Brutal Fruit);
  - avoid drinking in cars, including cars parked on a public road (WC s 80(1)(a)); avoid open cans in moving vehicles, boats under way, and drinking with sport or water sports;
  - never mock someone choosing the non-alc Margarita;
  - quantity-happiness lines ("more = happier") draw complaints even when cleared.

### Gaps
- The Brutal Fruit ruling text and clause basis were not obtained. The press said "illegal beach drinking" but did not cite which by-law.

## 4. Ages of people shown, and content appealing to minors

### Takeaway
The current DF-SA code has no general "must appear over 25" rule for everyone shown. That rule was in the 2020 Aware.org code. The current tiers are:
- paid influencers must "reasonably appear to be at least 25";
- celebrities (paid or unpaid) and on-site promoters must be, and appear to be, at least 21;
- people clearly visible in user-generated content (UGC) on brand pages must be, and appear to be, over the legal drinking age (18).

Creative with "primary appeal" to under-18s (cartoons, characters, youth music, slang and celebrities) is prohibited.

### Cited Findings
- **Primary appeal, DF-SA cl. 2.1.3:** "Commercial Communications may not use creative themes or elements that are intended to have primary appeal to people under the legal drinking age. "Primary appeal" means the creative themes or elements have a special attractiveness to people under the legal drinking age beyond the general attractiveness to persons of legal drinking age. Prohibited creative elements include cartoons, icons, characters, music, gestures, celebrities, and phrases or expressions known to be widely popular, primarily with people under the legal drinking age." — [DF-SA Code 2026](https://www.drinksfederationsa.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Code-of-Conduct-2026.pdf)
- **Celebrities, DF-SA cl. 2.1.6:** "The use of sport, music, film or television, or social media personalities ("celebrities"), whether paid or unpaid, in advertising or brand promotions, is acceptable if they are, and appear to a reasonable person to be, at least 21 years of age; shown to be behaving in an acceptable and responsible manner; and are in an age-appropriate setting. The use of celebrities who are at least 21 years of age but who appeal primarily to an underage audience is strictly prohibited, regardless of whether it is an alcohol or alcohol-free brand. No celebrities may be seen to be consuming alcohol, and if they specifically address their audience, their message must include a responsible drinking statement." — [DF-SA Code 2026](https://www.drinksfederationsa.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Code-of-Conduct-2026.pdf)
- **Paid influencers, DF-SA cl. 2.5.3.** Influencers "must: 2.5.3.1. Reasonably appear to be at least 25 years of age. 2.5.3.2. Not have disproportionate appeal to persons under the legal drinking age. 2.5.3.3. Demonstrate … no public association with irresponsible alcohol use or harmful drinking behaviours. 2.5.3.4. Have an audience composition where at least 70% of followers or viewers are confirmed to be above the legal drinking age, based on available platform insights or third-party verification tools." Under cl. 2.5.4.1, paid influencer content must not "Be directed at or feature pregnant women or persons under the legal drinking age." — [DF-SA Code 2026](https://www.drinksfederationsa.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Code-of-Conduct-2026.pdf)
- **UGC, DF-SA cl. 2.1.9 (rebuilt):** "Whenever user-generated content is on an alcohol brand-controlled website or social media page, all people who are clearly visible must be, and appear to a reasonable person to be, over the legal drinking age unless they are obviously incidental and in the background." — [DF-SA Code 2026](https://www.drinksfederationsa.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Code-of-Conduct-2026.pdf); ARB Appendix cl. 1.1.7 is identical. — [ARB Appendix Alcohol](https://arb.org.za/code-file-downloads/Appendix_Alcohol_v2026-02.pdf)
- **Promoters, DF-SA cl. 2.3.3:** "…all promotional team representatives must be, and appear to a reasonable person to be, at least 21 years of age." — [DF-SA Code 2026](https://www.drinksfederationsa.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Code-of-Conduct-2026.pdf)
- **Merchandise and sponsorship, DF-SA cl. 2.1.5:** sponsorship communications and promotional items "may not have primary appeal to people under the legal drinking age (e.g., children's toys)"; alcohol branding may not appear on sports apparel in children's sizes. — [DF-SA Code 2026](https://www.drinksfederationsa.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Code-of-Conduct-2026.pdf)
- **Youth culture, DF-SA cl. 7.10.3:** material "may not associate alcohol with celebrities under the legal drinking age, reflect the culture of that age group, or feature or portray actual or fictitious characters who are likely to appeal particularly to the legal drinking age in a way that might encourage them to drink." The phrase "particularly to the legal drinking age" is as written; it apparently means those under it. — [DF-SA Code 2026](https://www.drinksfederationsa.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Code-of-Conduct-2026.pdf)
- **The earlier 25 rule, Aware.org 2020 code:** "Any persons depicted in the act of drinking in commercial communication may not be, or appear to be, younger than 25 years of age." In an age-affirmed "controlled environment", people featured "may be younger than 25 years but they must be and reasonably appear to be at least 21 years old." — [Aware.org Code toolkit](https://assets-global.website-files.com/64afbf0da65d7b3be0358d54/64c9132703ce31f755a5ee3a_Code-Toolkit-1.pdf)
- **Liquor Act 59 of 2003, s 9(1)(a)(ii):** a person must not advertise liquor "in a manner intended to target or attract minors". — [SAFLII, Liquor Act 2003](https://www.saflii.org/za/legis/consol_act/la2003107/)
- **ARB guidance from the Don Billiato ruling.** Ads "could not feature celebrities at all under any circumstances, if the use therefore appeals to people under the drinking age, even if they were owners of the brand being featured." — [News24, 18 Jul 2024](https://www.news24.com/business/companies/very-much-a-party-vibe-arb-orders-cassper-nyovests-tequila-brand-to-pull-ad-glamourising-drinking-20240718)

### Inferences
- Treating 25+ (and looking 25+) as the casting floor for anyone shown drinking is the conservative house rule. It matches the paid-influencer rule and the older Aware.org rule, and it removes "appears 18" arguments. A strict reading of the current code would allow 21+ for celebrities and promoters and 18+ for UGC subjects.
- Before reposting UGC from Checkers shoppers, check that every clearly visible person plausibly looks 18+ (better, 25+).
- Avoid meme formats, trending audio or cartoon mascots that skew young. "Music" and "phrases or expressions" popular primarily with under-18s are named in cl. 2.1.3.

### Gaps
- I did not search the full 2026 text for a general minimum age for models in brand-produced content. The extracted text contains "25" only at cl. 2.5.3.1, so I found no general age floor. Confirm against the source PDF.

## 5. Digital and social media provisions: age-gating, UGC and reposting, influencers, moderation, responsibility messages, the "Not for Persons Under 18" line, alcohol content, non-alc products

### Takeaway
The code's digital rules are:
- brand-controlled social pages must be age-gated;
- content may be placed only where 70%+ of the audience is of legal drinking age;
- UGC on the brand page must be moderated "frequently" and non-compliant content removed "promptly";
- brands must take "reasonable steps" not to reshare non-compliant content, and must correct it within 24 hours of notification;
- every brand page and shareable asset needs a permanently visible responsibility message. On small-screen formats like Instagram, the "Not for Persons Under the Age of 18" token may stand in.

Two rules matter especially for Doña Fuego. Non-label communications must not feature alcohol content above 2.5% ABV, so "7%" should not be a selling point. And the non-alc Margarita shares the alcohol brand name, so the full code applies to it, without the "choose it when driving" positioning allowed to stand-alone alcohol-free brands.

### Inferences
- **Instagram account settings.** Set the account minimum age to 18 (see section 9). Put a permanent responsibility line in the bio. Burn the "Not for Persons Under the Age of 18" token into every feed post, story and reel frame. On longer video, add "Don't Drink and Drive" or "Pregnant Women Should Not Drink Alcohol" in the TV-style block. Where possible, set an auto-reply age disclaimer for new followers.
- **"7%".** Don't use it as a hook ("7% of pure fire") or as the subject of a post. A can whose label shows the ABV in a factual position is arguably the "label" exception, but a caption or graphic calling out 7% is not.
- **Non-alc Margarita.** It shares the "Doña Fuego" name, so:
  - the cl. 1.4 exclusion does not apply;
  - it can be promoted only to adults;
  - every alcohol rule applies;
  - the cl. 2.11.5 freedom to promote it "when expecting to drive" applies only to beverages that "do not carry an alcohol brand name".

  A "designated driver? grab the 0.0" line is therefore a risk under the strict text.
- **Reposts.** Each repost of a customer or influencer post becomes brand content and needs the token. Check the people's ages, the drinking behaviour and the captions before sharing. Fix flagged content within 24 hours.

### Gaps
- No primary text was found specifying whether Instagram Stories or Reels count as "small screen format" for the token alternative. The code names "Instagram or Twitter" generally.
- The DF-SA's approved font/size rules for the token on stories (as opposed to "the advertisement" generally) were not found beyond the 15%-block spec.

## 6. Promotions and competitions: code rules plus Consumer Protection Act s 36 and regulation 11

### Takeaway
An Instagram giveaway by an alcohol brand must:
- exclude under-18s (and should say so explicitly);
- not encourage excessive consumption;
- charge no entry fee and no price premium (electronic entry costs are capped at R1.50);
- have written rules prepared before launch and available free on request;
- state the six s 36(5) items in the offer;
- exclude employees, agents, suppliers and their families;
- be overseen and certified by an independent accountant, auditor, attorney or advocate;
- keep records for at least 3 years;
- not force winners into publicity.

TikTok ads must not offer alcohol as a prize.

### Cited Findings
- **DF-SA cl. 2.3.1:** "No brand promotions, events or competitions related to an alcohol brand should be directed primarily at persons under the legal drinking age, nor may any brand promotions, events or competitions encourage the rapid, irresponsible, or excessive consumption of alcohol". — [DF-SA Code 2026](https://www.drinksfederationsa.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Code-of-Conduct-2026.pdf)
- **DF-SA cl. 2.3.2:** "It should be stated explicitly that persons under the legal drinking age are ineligible to participate in events and competitions aimed at promoting an alcohol brand and/or product." — [DF-SA Code 2026](https://www.drinksfederationsa.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Code-of-Conduct-2026.pdf). The ARB Appendix has only cl. 1.3.1: "No adverts for competitions related to an alcohol brand should be directed primarily at persons under the legal drinking age." — [ARB Appendix Alcohol](https://arb.org.za/code-file-downloads/Appendix_Alcohol_v2026-02.pdf)
- **CPA s 36(1)(d):** "promotional competition" means any competition, game, scheme, arrangement, system, plan or device for distributing prizes by lot or chance, if it promotes a business or the sale of goods and "any prize offered exceeds the threshold prescribed", "irrespective of whether a participant is required to demonstrate any skill or ability before being awarded a prize." — [SAFLII, CPA 2008](https://www.saflii.org/za/legis/consol_act/cpa2008246/)
- **CPA s 36(3)(a):** the promoter "must not require any consideration to be paid by or on behalf of any participant in the promotional competition, other than the reasonable costs of posting or otherwise transmitting an entry form or device". — [SAFLII, CPA](https://www.saflii.org/za/legis/consol_act/cpa2008246/)
- **CPA s 36(4):** consideration is deemed required if a participant must pay "for the opportunity to participate … for access to the competition or for any device", or if a required purchase is priced "more than the price, excluding discounts, ordinarily charged … without the opportunity of taking part". — [SAFLII, CPA](https://www.saflii.org/za/legis/consol_act/cpa2008246/)
- **CPA s 36(3)(b).** The promoter must not award a prize "(i) a winner of the competition if it is unlawful to supply those goods or services to that prize winner". Nor may it award one to "(aa) a director, member, partner, employee or agent of, or consultant to the promoter or any other person who directly or indirectly controls or is controlled by, the promoter; or (bb) a supplier of goods or services in connection with that competition". — [SAFLII, CPA](https://www.saflii.org/za/legis/consol_act/cpa2008246/)
- **CPA s 36(3)(c):** the promoter must "(i) prepare competition rules before the beginning of the competition; (ii) make the competition rules available to the Commission and to any participant, on request and without cost; and (iii) retain a copy of the competition rules for the prescribed period". — [SAFLII, CPA](https://www.saflii.org/za/legis/consol_act/cpa2008246/)
- **CPA s 36(5):** "An offer to participate in a promotional competition must clearly state— (a) the benefit or competition to which the offer relates; (b) the steps required by a person to accept the offer or to participate in the competition; (c) the basis on which the results of the competition will be determined; (d) the closing date for the competition; (e) the medium through or by which the results of the competition will be made known; and (f) any person from whom, any place where, and any date and time on or at which— (i) a person may obtain a copy of the competition rules; and (ii) a successful participant may receive any prize." Under s 36(6), these may be stated directly on the participation medium, on an accompanying document, or in a clearly associated advertisement. — [SAFLII, CPA](https://www.saflii.org/za/legis/consol_act/cpa2008246/)
- **CPA s 36(8)–(9):** the right to a prize "is fully vested immediately upon the determination of the results" and must not be "made subject to any further condition" or made contingent on payment or extra requirements beyond those stated under s 36(5). — [SAFLII, CPA](https://www.saflii.org/za/legis/consol_act/cpa2008246/)
- **Reg 11(1) and 11(4) (GNR.293 of 1 April 2011).** The reasonable cost of electronically transmitting an entry "shall not exceed R1.50", including "the total cost of all subsequent communication". The prize threshold is R1.00. — [CDH, 22 Jan 2020](https://www.cliffedekkerhofmeyr.com/news/publications/2020/corporate/corporate-and-commercial-alert-22-january-Rules-of-the-game-Keep-the-consumer-protection-act-in-mind-when-facilitating-promotional-competitions.html); [Polity, 2011](https://www.polity.org.za/article/promotional-competitions-and-their-requirements-under-the-consumer-protection-act-2008-2011-06-06) (secondary sources; regulation text not read directly)
- **Reg 11(3):** a rule requiring a winner to permit use of their image, take part in marketing, or attend the draw "without affording the prize winner the opportunity to decline … is void." — [CDH](https://www.cliffedekkerhofmeyr.com/news/publications/2020/corporate/corporate-and-commercial-alert-22-january-Rules-of-the-game-Keep-the-consumer-protection-act-in-mind-when-facilitating-promotional-competitions.html) (secondary)
- **Reg 11(5):** "the promoter must ensure that an independent accountant, registered auditor, attorney or advocate oversees and certifies the conducting of the competition". — [CDH](https://www.cliffedekkerhofmeyr.com/news/publications/2020/corporate/corporate-and-commercial-alert-22-january-Rules-of-the-game-Keep-the-consumer-protection-act-in-mind-when-facilitating-promotional-competitions.html) (secondary)
- **Reg 11(6), records for at least 3 years,** including:
  - "a list of all instances when the promotional competition was marketed, including details on the dates, the medium used and places";
  - a winner's signed acknowledgment of receipt with ID number and date;
  - declarations under oath that winners were not directors, employees, agents, consultants or marketing service providers, "or the spouses, life partners, business partners or immediate family members";
  - the basis for determining winners;
  - a summary of the draw proceedings.
  — [CDH](https://www.cliffedekkerhofmeyr.com/news/publications/2020/corporate/corporate-and-commercial-alert-22-january-Rules-of-the-game-Keep-the-consumer-protection-act-in-mind-when-facilitating-promotional-competitions.html) (secondary)
- **Prize redemption restrictions.** Spoor & Fisher (7 Aug 2026) say a promoter may not restrict redemption without "at least 20 business days' written notice", and restricted periods "may not exceed 90 days in any calendar year". Winners may decline publicity, since "The brand has no inherent right to a winner's name, image or likeness". POPIA applies to entry data. — [Spoor & Fisher, 7 Aug 2026](https://spoor.com/the-law-behind-promotional-competition/)
- **Unlawful supply to minors.** Liquor Act s 10(1): "A person must not sell or supply liquor or methylated spirits to a minor." — [SAFLII, Liquor Act](https://www.saflii.org/za/legis/consol_act/la2003107/). Western Cape Liquor Act s 54(2): "No person may give or supply liquor to a person under the age of eighteen (18) years…" — [WC Liquor Act](https://www.sawis.co.za/winelaw/download/Western_Cape_Liquor_Act,_as_amended.pdf)
- **TikTok.** Its alcohol market-requirements page (search excerpt) states: "Ads must not offer alcohol as a prize or reward, or promote any offers or incentives…". — [TikTok Ads: Alcohol market-specific requirements](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/alcohol-market-specific-requirements) (search-result excerpt only; full general-requirements text not captured)

### Inferences
- **Who may enter.** Make 18+ (with an ID check at prize handover) an explicit entry condition. The CPA forbids awarding a prize that is unlawful to supply to the winner, and supplying liquor to a minor is unlawful under both Acts.
- **Mechanics and costs.** "Like, comment, tag a friend, follow" mechanics carry no payment, so they fit within s 36(3). A "buy a 4-pack at Checkers to enter" mechanic is lawful only if there is no price uplift.
- **Content of the mechanic.** Avoid mechanics that reward the volume of drinking ("show us your 10 empties") or tie prizes to consumption feats, which breaches cl. 2.3.1. Tagging friends who may be minors is a risk under cl. 2.3.1 and 2.1.11.
- **Rules and notices.** Publish the full terms (link in bio) and state the six s 36(5) items in the caption or on a linked page. Appoint an independent attorney or accountant to certify the draw. Keep the reg 11(6) file for 3 years.
- **Platform and retailer terms.** Meta's own promotions terms (release of Meta, etc.) and any Checkers/Sixty60 co-promotion terms apply on top. These were not researched.

### Gaps
- The text of regulation 11 itself was not read; the wording comes from law-firm commentary (CDH, Polity, Spoor & Fisher).
- Meta's Pages/Instagram promotion guidelines were not retrieved.
- It is not established whether alcohol as a prize on organic Instagram posts is restricted by Meta. TikTok's no-alcohol-prize rule applies to ads.

## 7. ARB Code of Advertising Practice: alcohol-specific rules, incorporation of the industry code, jurisdiction over non-members, recent alcohol rulings

### Takeaway
The ARB Code covers alcohol through its "Alcohol" appendix, which reproduces the DF-SA Alcohol Industry Code of Conduct. The current file is Appendix_Alcohol_v2026-02, headed "(2025)". Recent alcohol rulings include Don Billiato (2024), Brutal Fruit (2025), Strongbow (2025), Solstice Seltzer (2026) and Wildebeest (2026). Detail of each is in sections 2, 3 and 5.

## 8. Statute: Liquor Act s 9, Western Cape Liquor Act, health-message regulations, and pending bills

### Takeaway
National law is thin. Liquor Act 59 of 2003 s 9 bars advertising liquor "in a false or misleading manner" or "in a manner intended to target or attract minors", and no national alcohol-advertising regulations were found. The Western Cape Liquor Act 4 of 2008 has no content rules for advertising: s 58(2) lets licensees advertise, and s 54 prohibits supplying under-18s. The rotating health messages come from 2007 regulations under the Foodstuffs, Cosmetics and Disinfectants Act, and they apply to container labels, not ads. A 2017 draft replacement was withdrawn in 2020.

Proposed, not enacted:
- the EFF private member's Liquor Amendment Bill B21-2025, introduced 8 September 2025, which would ban all liquor advertising, promotion, brand-element use and sponsorship. Parliament still lists it at the "Introduction" stage.
- the 2013 Control of Marketing of Alcoholic Beverages Bill, approved by Cabinet for comment in 2013 but never enacted;
- the 2016 draft Liquor Amendment Bill, also stalled.

### Cited Findings
- **Liquor Act 59 of 2003, s 9 (current):** "(1) A person must not advertise— (a) any liquor or methylated spirits— (i) in a false or misleading manner; (ii) in a manner intended to target or attract minors; or (b) any substance that is prohibited in terms of this Act. (2) A person must not advertise any substance as liquor or methylated spirits if that substance is not liquor or methylated spirits, respectively, as defined in this Act." — [SAFLII, Liquor Act 2003](https://www.saflii.org/za/legis/consol_act/la2003107/); [gov.za PDF](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201409/a59-03.pdf)
- **Liquor Act s 10(1):** "A person must not sell or supply liquor or methylated spirits to a minor." — [SAFLII](https://www.saflii.org/za/legis/consol_act/la2003107/)
- **Western Cape Liquor Act 4 of 2008 (as amended by Act 10 of 2010):**
  - s 58(2): "Notwithstanding the provisions of this section, a licensee may advertise the sale of liquor and solicit and receive orders for the purchase of liquor elsewhere than on the licensed premises." This is the only "advertise" provision apart from licence-application notices.

### Inferences
- **"False or misleading" in s 9(1)(a)(i).** This is the most plausible statutory hook for a claim that alcohol fixes or cures something. In practice, enforcement against social posts runs through the ARB, not the Liquor Act.
- **Health messages on social.** The seven health messages are legally required on labels, not on social posts. On social, the code's responsibility messages (Don't Drink and Drive / Not for Persons Under the Age of 18 / Pregnant Women Should Not Drink Alcohol) are what's required.
- **B21-2025.** It is not law. If it progressed, it would end brand social media for alcohol entirely, including the Doña Fuego name on the non-alc product's promotion, since "brand element" is caught. It is worth monitoring, but it has not moved past introduction in 13 months.

- The current (post-2010) consolidated Western Cape Liquor Act was not obtained. Later amendments, such as a Western Cape Liquor Amendment Act of 2015, may add provisions not in the SAWIS copy.
- The full body of R764/2007 (placement, rotation, size) was not read. I did not verify whether any provision extends the messages to advertisements.
- No 2025–2026 government (DTIC or Health) announcement on reviving the 2013 or 2016 bills was found.

## 9. Platform policies: Meta (Facebook/Instagram) and TikTok in South Africa

### Takeaway
- **Meta paid ads:** alcohol ads are permitted in South Africa only if they comply with local law and "required or established industry codes", and are targeted to 18+.

- **TikTok paid ads:** alcohol ads may be allowed in South Africa only through a TikTok sales representative, with registration documents, disclaimers and 18+ targeting. Bars and alcohol delivery services may not be advertised.

## 10. Guidance on specific words: "fix", "need", "therapy", "self-care", "survive", "cure", "earned it"

### Takeaway
No South African code, ruling or regulator guidance found names these words. The applicable tests are principle-based:
- cl. 2.4.2: no "solve personal problems" or enabling success or acceptance;
- cl. 2.7.4: no "curative qualities", "stimulant, sedative, or tranquiliser";
- cl. 2.7.3: no health claims;
- cl. 2.8.2: no glamourising "drug culture";
- the preamble's requirement to follow the "letter and spirit".

The closest enforcement precedents are Strongbow (implied relief from work stress, upheld) and Don Billiato ("taste of wealth", social success, upheld). The Wildebeest ruling shows the limit: positive associations are fine unless they "explicitly promise success or claim to solve personal problems".

### Cited Findings
- **"Cure", "therapy" and "medicine"** map onto DF-SA cl. 2.7.4 ("curative qualities … sedative, or tranquiliser") and cl. 2.7.3 (no "health claims", "healthier", "better for you", "safer"). — [DF-SA Code 2026](https://www.drinksfederationsa.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Code-of-Conduct-2026.pdf)
- **"Survive Monday", "stress", "need" and "fix"** map onto DF-SA cl. 2.4.2 ("solve personal problems, overcome inhibitions, shyness, or social barriers") and the Strongbow ruling (relief from workplace stress). — [DF-SA Code 2026](https://www.drinksfederationsa.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Code-of-Conduct-2026.pdf); [News24 Strongbow](https://www.news24.com/business/consumer-lookout/strongbow-ad-promotes-booze-to-help-manage-work-stress-watchdog-finds-20250605-0715)
- **"Fix" in dictionary usage** denotes a dose of a drug or a substance that is needed: "He was shaking badly and needed a fix." — [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/fix). Drug culture is barred by DF-SA cl. 2.8.2, and brand names and packaging may not convey "alcohol abuse" or "drug culture" under cl. 2.10.1. — [DF-SA Code 2026](https://www.drinksfederationsa.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Code-of-Conduct-2026.pdf)
- **"Earned it" or "reward"** map onto DF-SA cl. 2.4.2's success limb ("business, social, professional, academic, athletic, financial, or other personal success") and cl. 2.5.4.5 for influencers. No ruling on reward framing was found. — [DF-SA Code 2026](https://www.drinksfederationsa.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Code-of-Conduct-2026.pdf)
- **Humour as mitigation.** It saved Wildebeest's quantity-happiness line in 2026 but did not save Don Billiato's "taste of wealth" ("tongue in cheek" defence) in 2024. — [Bizcommunity](https://www.bizcommunity.com/article/arb-clears-wildebeest-brandewyn-ad-accused-of-promoting-binge-drinking-067655a); [News24](https://www.news24.com/business/companies/very-much-a-party-vibe-arb-orders-cassper-nyovests-tequila-brand-to-pull-ad-glamourising-drinking-20240718)

### Inferences
- **The red-flag test** for the one-pager: does the caption position the drink as the answer to a lack or problem? That includes a need, craving, stress, a bad day, shyness, loneliness, status or reward for suffering. If so, rewrite it so the drink accompanies a good moment that people create themselves (togetherness, food, music, an occasion). Don't make it the cause or the cure.
- **Safer swaps for "get your Fuego fix":**
  - "Pour a Fuego for the table"
  - "Fuego for the braai"
  - "Pick up a 4-pack at Checkers"
  - "Margarita, Spicy Margarita or Paloma, which is yours?"

  These are copy suggestions, not legal clearance.
